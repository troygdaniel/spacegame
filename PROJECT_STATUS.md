## In one minute

A browser game, plain JavaScript on Phaser 3, no build step. Two modes on one title screen:

- **The Campaign** (where all current work is): a Homeworld-style *commander* game. You fly a big, slow carrier ("the cruiser") and command its small ships (fighters, escorts) with two short order lists, a command strip and your voice. Missions are scripted like X-Wing's (a mission is a sequence of beats with objectives and bonuses), and the ship's power is shared between weapons, shields and engines like X-Wing's. Earth's fleet against the aliens: three levels, nine missions, a story about an outpost, not an invasion.
- **Long Haul** (finished, not being changed): an Elite-flavoured trader and rescue game. It shares the engine, the art and the sounds.

The owner is Troy. He plays the build himself and gives feedback in chat; his feedback becomes the work. His standing goal for the Campaign (21 Nov 2026): *fights were not interactive and escorts were boring; he wants to direct the fleet more.* Feature freeze since 20 Nov: **bug fixes and the fleet-command batch only; new ideas go on the bench** (`ROADMAP.md`).

## What works now, level by level

All nine missions are playable and winnable (the plain test bot wins every one; a player who gives no orders does not). Numbers and detail: `docs/CAMPAIGN.md`, balance: `docs/CAMPAIGN-TUNING.md`.

| Level | Missions | State |
|---|---|---|
| **1, the rim run** (Lunar Base to Ceres and back toward Earth) | 1.1 escort a transport past pirates, 1.2 mine at Ceres and defend the miners, 1.3 escort the haulers home and meet the first aliens | The newest and most worked level. **1.1 is the one finished example** of the fleet-command systems (below): something happens about every minute. 1.2 and 1.3 are tuned (the rim tune; then a hunt tune: 1.2's goal is 450 ore of harder rock, so mining takes most of its five minutes while the raids land, and 1.3's first contact is eight ships, not ten, so one wrong answer costs a few ships, not eight) but the new beats are not scripted into them. Then the **Earth refit** (pick one of three techs, spend ore on ships). |
| **2, Alpha Centauri** (warp unlocked) | 2.1 resources, 2.2 the guards, 2.3 the relic | Works. Being retuned for the new systems. |
| **3, the alien home world** | 3.1 approach, 3.2 outer ring, 3.3 inner ring (twelve towers) | Works, then the ending (the outpost reveal). Being retuned for the new systems. |

Between missions you are in **the ready room**: four rows (Missions, Refit, Rank, Launch). Enter opens a row as an overlay (missions with their briefing, the refit with what each choice changes in numbers, the rank ladder). You can replay any mission you have flown, and fly any mission an older save left skipped (`[-]`); the last row of the Missions list starts the campaign over (it asks first). After each battle you pick one of three techs.

### What the fleet-command batch added (all on main)

The batch is the answer to "I want to direct the fleet more". Each item has its own section in `docs/CAMPAIGN.md`.

- **Short lists with shortcut keys** (cards `one-list-keys`, Troy, 22 Nov 2026, and `list-shortcuts`, Troy, 23 Nov: "shortcut keys that take you straight to the sub-list"; they replaced the two always-on panels). Nothing is on the screen until a list's key is pressed, and only one list is open at a time. **Left hand, the fleet:** `Q` the fleet list (a row for each part: All squads, Red, Green, Yellow, Escorts, Convoy, Stance; Launch, Guard, Mine and Recall are in All squads, nothing is listed twice), `W` all squads, `E` the red squad, `R` the green squad, `T` the yellow squad (see the next bullet); Escorts, Convoy and Stance have no key: Enter on their row in `Q` opens them. **Right hand, the ship, mirrored:** `P` the ship list, `O` autopilot (the goal, the target, the two fast burns), `I` power (a pip to shields, engines or weapons, balance, emergency shields), `U` dock and build. **A part's key opens it directly at any time** (`W` `Enter`: every fighter attacks the target); `Enter`, `Right` or a click on a part's row opens it too; `Left` goes back. In a list `Up` and `Down` move the highlight and `Enter` gives the order: the list closes when the order is taken (a power row stays open, to press again), a refusal leaves it open and says why; the list's key closes it, and **`Esc` goes back one step, to where you came from** (a list opened from a row of `Q` or `P` goes back to it; a list opened by its own key closes; with nothing open it pauses). **An order that needs a target helps pick it** (card `space-game-order-target-pick`, Troy, 22 Nov 2026): `Attack`, `Escort the convoy` and `Mine ore` take the one candidate at once, or, with several, open a sub-list of them (a hostile, a convoy ship or a miner, a rock field) with the highlight shown in the flight view (a bracket, and the view zooms out to show it with the cruiser, then back); `Enter` chooses, `Esc` goes back to the list. A target already picked with `A` `D` `S` is attacked at once; with no hostile `Attack` is grey ("none seen"). Details: `docs/CAMPAIGN.md`, "Picking the target of an order". A row that cannot be used now is grey with the reason; a row that can never be used in the mission is left out. **No key is reused in flight and there are no number keys.** The other direct keys: the arrows fly, `Space` the turrets, `Enter` a missile (only while no list is open), `A D S X` the one target (its name is under the radar), `J K L ;` power (the same orders as the `I` list), `Tab` the map (in it `Up` `Down` step the marks, `Enter` flies, `Shift+Enter` fast, `V` scouts, `X` clears the trip), `- = Backspace` zoom, `H`, `M`, `Esc`. The same orders can be typed (`/`) or spoken (hold `.`). The key table is `src/data_controls.js`; every hint reads it.
- **Fighter squads** (card `space-game-fighter-squads`, Troy, 22 Nov 2026: "I want to be able to control fighter squads easily"). The fighters are split into three squads, **Red, Green and Yellow**: the starting six leave the bay two and two and two; a fighter built, ranked or refitted later fills the thinnest squad; a squad that lost its ships is filled by the next to leave; a fighter mended aboard goes out in its own squad. **`W` all squads** (Launch the wing, Attack the target, Guard the cruiser, Recall, Mine ore: it opens on Launch while ships are in the bay and on Attack once the wing is out, so `W`, Enter and `Q`, Enter, Enter launch everything), **`E` red, `R` green, `T` yellow** (Attack the target, Guard the cruiser, Recall). An order to one squad leaves the others on theirs, so two squads can hold two targets. A squad with no ship out greys its list ("none out") and says so in words. Each fighter is drawn in its squad's colour in flight and on the map (the legend names the squads); there is no circle in normal flight: a circle shows round a squad's ships only while that squad's list is open (`E` `R` `T`, or all three for `W`; card `space-game-squad-tint`, Troy, 22 Nov 2026). The escorts are in no squad (their orders are `Q`, Escorts), and **convoy orders are `Q`, Convoy** (`W` is no longer the convoy's key; a hint says "Q, Convoy"). The fleet list has seven rows (All squads, Red, Green, Yellow, Escorts, Convoy, Stance; six with no convoy): Launch the wing, Guard, Mine and Recall left the top level and live in All squads (card `space-game-squad-tint`, Troy, 22 Nov 2026: "nothing listed twice"). "Red squad attack the raider", "yellow guard", "all squads recall" work typed and spoken. The bots order the whole wing and win as before. (Assumptions: three squads even in a small wing; no way yet to move a fighter between squads; the squad colour is a tint on the fighter and the circle shows only while its list is open; on the map the dot has a white spark so it is told from an alien's or a pirate's.)
- **No command time.** The battle never changes speed because of the lists: while an order list (`Q` or `P`), a shortcut sub-list, the map or a pilot's call is open, and after an order, it runs at its own speed. There is no sign and no hint about it. (Card `space-game-no-command-slowdown`, 22 Nov: Troy said "do not change the speed of the game with command mode"; it took out the quarter-speed time that card `space-game-command-slowtime` had built.) While a list or a call has the arrows the cruiser coasts. Fights are 15 percent slower overall than they were at first (Calm 0.6, Normal 0.72, Brisk 0.85; that is the pace, kept), and the Campaign has one pace (Normal): the P key and the pace setting are gone.
- **Pilots ask for orders.** Timed radio calls with three choices, real costs and effects. The call is a small amber list low in the middle: `Up` `Down` pick, `Enter` answers, and the highlight starts on the pilot's own cautious pick (what he does if you say nothing). It waits, dimmed, while a `Q` or `P` list or the map is open. **A press of `Up` or `Down` stops the pilot's 8-second timer** (the bar stays where it was and turns pale blue, the key line says "Timer stopped"), and the call waits for `Enter`; untouched it runs out to the cautious pick as before. No other key stops it. A held call gives up after 30 s (`CAMP_CALLS.held`: the pilot takes the cautious pick and says so), so it cannot block the next one for ever. (Card `space-game-call-timer-stops`, Troy, 22 Nov 2026.) **The 1.1 distress call offers the squads** (card `space-game-distress-send-squads`, Troy, 22 Nov 2026: "for the miner we help in mission 1.1, let him send squads, all or specific"): five rows, **Send all squads, Send Red squad, Send Green squad, Send Yellow squad, Stay with the transport** (the cautious one). The squad sent flies to the miner, fights the skiffs there and goes back to the order it had when they are dead or gone; the others keep theirs, the escorts stay with the transport. A squad with no ship out is grey with "none out", and Enter on it says why and leaves the call open (the timer rule is as before: `Up` or `Down` stops it). The plate grows to five rows from the same bottom edge. In the plain flight (seeds 1 to 3, answered 2 s after the call, when the miner has 22 hit points and loses 0.8 a second): all squads save the miner every time (4 to 10 hit points left, the pirates dead in 18 to 23 s); one squad can do it but slowly (Red 3 of 3 in 16 to 31 s, Green 2 of 3, Yellow 1 of 3: about two in three); staying loses it every time. Sending all is rated a wrong answer (the wing is rattled for 20 s: the transport has no fighters about it), one squad a right one. The plain (thinking) bot answers Send Red and still wins. Any call can offer squads: `campCallSquadFx` in `src/camporders.js`. (Assumptions: "all squads" is the three fighter squads, the escorts are not sent; one squad is the rated-right answer; the clock is not stopped while a refused row is shown.)
- **Convoy orders.** Tuck in, Hold here, Run for it, for the ships you escort; the cruiser's shield can cover a ship tucked in.
- **Mission 1.1 is one road** (card `space-game-cut-lane-fork`, Troy, 22 Nov 2026: "remove picking a lane, not feeling it"). There is no fork, no hold for a word, no lane rows or scouting of lanes on the Tab map, no T key and no lane hints: the transport flies the road and the ambush with the torpedo boats happens on it, at the same place and time as it did on the short lane (the long lane, its two small raids and its cache of ore went away). The route system (`src/camproute.js`, `tests/camproute.mjs`) stays in the code, dormant: a mission row that writes a `route` still gets a fork. Scouts still read places in the missions that have recon or a cache to fetch (V on the Tab map).
- **Enemy torpedo boats** (slow, ignore the fighters, launch a heavy torpedo at the convoy; the turrets and missiles can shoot torpedoes down) and a **rival ship** (the pirate leader: an enemy your size that duels the cruiser in phases, so power management matters).
- **Fighter repair.** A hurt fighter flies home, is mended and relaunches.
- **Ship systems.** Power pips to weapons, shields and engines (keys J K L, `;` to reset); three Long Haul-style gauges (shields, speed, lasers) with power bars, **at the top right** (card `space-game-power-presets`, Troy, 22 Nov 2026: the ship lives on the right hand, with the `P` list), and **the power list (`I`) opens right under them**: its Shields, Engines and Weapons rows each carry that system's pip bar, the twin of the gauge's (one column, under the weapons gauge's bar, the same colour and the same animation, so both light together when Enter gives a pip), the highlighted row frames its gauge with a line down to the list, and a system at four pips greys its row with "at full"; `J` `K` `L` are still one pip a press and `;` balances (Troy turned an "all power in one press" idea down: pips move one at a time); **shields now mend by themselves**, at a rate the shield pips set (the old pay-ore recharge is gone).
- **The briefing of mission 1.1 is the map on every page, with your ships walking the plan** (card `space-game-briefing-map-walk`, Troy, 22 Nov 2026: "the mission map should be used for each page of the briefing, zoomed as needed, with my own craft walking through the plan"). Opening 1.1's briefing from the board shows the map under the heading on all three pages; the cruiser, the wing and the transport walk the road from Luna to Ceres and stop at six points (leave Luna with the patrol, the patrol turns back, a distress call may come, pirates are likely to ambush the road, the pirate leader waits near Ceres, arrive), the map zooms to each, and one line of words sits under it. A page plays by itself and waits at its last stop; `Enter` or `Right` goes on, `Left` back, `Enter` at the very end or `L` launches, `Esc` closes, `+` `-` `Backspace` and the wheel zoom by hand. Dangers are dashed hint rings ("PIRATES?"), never a list of ships. The story, the goals and bonus, and the keys and readiness are small strips under the map (one per page). The other eight missions keep the three-screen briefing; a mission gets a walk by a row of data (`src/data_briefwalk.js`) and its lines. `M` does nothing on a walking map.
- **The tuning panel** (card `space-game-tuning-panel`, Troy, 22 Nov 2026: the way he iterates on feel from now on). `N`, in flight and in the ready room, opens a list of numbers and on/off switches for whole systems; the battle waits while it is open. `Up` `Down` choose, `Left` `Right` change (`Shift` ten steps), `Backspace` puts a row back, `Esc` closes. Each row says when its change is felt (at once, next mission, next call). The changes are kept in this browser (localStorage), a **TUNED** mark shows while any is on, and a mission flown tuned keeps no score and no rank (the level still moves on). **Copy my settings** puts only the changed rows on the clipboard; `node tools/apply-tuning.mjs` reads that block and prints the `CAMP` changes it means (`--write` bakes them in as the new defaults). Bots, node tests and boot scripts never see overrides: only a scene reads the file, and a driven browser (`navigator.webdriver`) neither reads nor writes it. The rows are one table, `CAMP_TUNE` in `src/camptune.js`; the switches are `CAMP.flags`, read through `campFlag`. **Rows:** 23 numbers (the cruiser's speed, turn, shield size and mend rate; the turrets' damage, delay and reach; missiles and the laser charge; fighter hull and damage; convoy speed; the enemy's hull and damage; battle pace, time to the waves, the pilot call timer and gap, the starting zoom; ore per rock and mining rate) and 10 switches (pilots' calls, the 1.1 distress call, torpedo boats, the rival, fighter repair, convoy orders, stances, turrets firing by themselves, the laser charge, the hints); the plain bot still wins 1.1 with each switch off and with all off together. Details and the table: `docs/CAMPAIGN-TUNING.md`.
- **Sensors** (card `space-game-see-hostiles-earlier`). The cruiser sees a hostile out to 9,000 px (`CAMP.sensor.range`; 21 s of the fastest raider's flight, 35 s of a drone's; a scout out sees further, inside its own circle): on the radar, the Tab map, the nav list and the target keys (`A` `D` `S`). A hostile off the screen gets a red **edge arrow** (how many, what, how far), a group that has just come into sight pulses on the radar, and the radio calls it once ("Contact, east: 3 hostiles, 520 km.", with the kinds named when a scout, an escort or the cruiser is within 2,600 px of them). The range is a row of the `N` tuning panel (0 sees only the mothership and the towers). Waves spawn when and as strong as before: what changed is what the player is told, not when the enemy comes (most waves still start 3,000 to 5,000 px out, so the warning is the flight from there: 2 to 9 s for the fast ones, up to 37 s for the far ones).
- **Quality of life.** Zoom out to the whole system (Tab map) and, in flight, out to where a mission's whole route fits on the screen (1.1's is 16,500 px: its farthest zoom is 0.021 and the names of the places and the convoy are drawn as marks there; a short mission's is 0.11); the ore store holds 500; the title starts a mode only on a deliberate choice (Enter, K, arrows then Enter, or a click), not on any key. **The stars follow the zoom** (card `space-game-stars-at-zoom`, Troy, 22 Nov 2026: zoomed out, the starfield streamed past places that stand still, like Ceres or the miner): the three star layers move in proportion to the wheel's zoom (up to 1, so zoomed in and in cruise nothing changed), a fixed place keeps up best with the near layer, and at a long mission's farthest zoom the stars hardly move; `tests/starszoom.mjs`.
