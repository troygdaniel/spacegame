## In one minute

A browser game, plain JavaScript on Phaser 3, no build step. Two modes on one title screen:

- **The Campaign** (where all current work is): a Homeworld-style *commander* game. You fly a big, slow carrier ("the cruiser") and command its small ships (fighters, escorts) with two short order lists, a command strip and your voice. Missions are scripted like X-Wing's (a mission is a sequence of beats with objectives and bonuses), and the ship's power is shared between weapons, shields and engines like X-Wing's. Earth's fleet against the aliens: three levels, nine missions, a story about an outpost, not an invasion.
- **Long Haul** (finished, not being changed): an Elite-flavoured trader and rescue game. It shares the engine, the art and the sounds.

The owner is Troy. He plays the build himself and gives feedback in chat; his feedback becomes the work. His standing goal for the Campaign (21 Nov 2026): *fights were not interactive and escorts were boring; he wants to direct the fleet more.* Feature freeze since 20 Nov: **bug fixes and the fleet-command batch only; new ideas go on the bench** (`ROADMAP.md`).

## What works now, level by level

All nine missions are playable and winnable (the plain test bot wins every one; a player who gives no orders does not). Numbers and detail: `docs/CAMPAIGN.md`, balance: `docs/CAMPAIGN-TUNING.md`.

| Level | Missions | State |
|---|---|---|
| **1, the rim run** (Lunar Base to Ceres and back toward Earth) | 1.1 escort a transport past pirates, 1.2 mine at Ceres and defend the miners, 1.3 escort the haulers home and meet the first aliens | The newest and most worked level. **1.1 is the one finished example** of the fleet-command systems (below): something happens about every minute. 1.2 and 1.3 are tuned (the rim tune; then a hunt tune: 1.2's goal is 450 ore of harder rock, so mining takes most of its five minutes while the raids land, and 1.3's first contact is eight ships, not ten, so one wrong answer costs a few ships, not eight) but the new beats are not scripted into them. Between missions you are in **the ready room**: four rows (Missions, Refit, Rank, Launch). Enter opens a row as an overlay (missions with their briefing, the refit with what each choice changes in numbers, the rank ladder). You can replay any mission you have flown. After each battle you pick one of three techs.

### What the fleet-command batch added (all on main)

The batch is the answer to "I want to direct the fleet more". Each item has its own section in `docs/CAMPAIGN.md`.

- **Short lists with shortcut keys** (cards `one-list-keys`, Troy, 22 Nov 2026, and `list-shortcuts`, Troy, 23 Nov: "shortcut keys that take you straight to the sub-list"; they replaced the two always-on panels). Nothing is on the screen until a list's key is pressed, and only one list is open at a time. **Left hand, the fleet:** `Q` the fleet list (Launch the wing, Guard the cruiser, Mine ore, Recall the wing, and a row for each part), `W` convoy orders, `E` fighters (Attack the target, Guard, Mine, Recall the fighters), `R` escorts (Escort the convoy, Attack, Guard, Recall the escorts); Stance is a part with no key. **Right hand, the ship, mirrored:** `P` the ship list, `O` autopilot (the goal, the target, the two fast burns), `I` power (a pip to shields, engines or weapons, balance, emergency shields), `U` dock and build. **A part's key opens it directly at any time** (`E` `Enter`: the fighters attack the target); `Enter`, `Right` or a click on a part's row opens it too; `Left` goes back. In a list `Up` and `Down` move the highlight and `Enter` gives the order: the list closes when the order is taken (a power row stays open, to press again), a refusal leaves it open and says why; the list's key or `Esc` closes it. A row that cannot be used now is grey with the reason; a row that can never be used in the mission is left out. **No key is reused in flight and there are no number keys.** The other direct keys: the arrows fly, `Space` the turrets, `Enter` a missile (only while no list is open), `A D S X` the one target (its name is under the radar), `J K L ;` power (the same orders as the `I` list), `Tab` the map (in it `Up` `Down` step the marks, `Enter` flies, `Shift+Enter` fast, `V` scouts, `T` takes a lane, `X` clears the trip), `- = Backspace` zoom, `H`, `M`, `Esc`. The same orders can be typed (`/`) or spoken (hold `.`). The key table is `src/data_controls.js`; every hint reads it.
- **No command time.** The battle never changes speed because of the lists: while an order list (`Q` or `P`), a shortcut sub-list, the map or a pilot's call is open, and after an order, it runs at its own speed. There is no sign and no hint about it. (Card `space-game-no-command-slowdown`, 22 Nov: Troy said "do not change the speed of the game with command mode"; it took out the quarter-speed time that card `space-game-command-slowtime` had built.) While a list or a call has the arrows the cruiser coasts. Fights are 15 percent slower overall than they were at first (Calm 0.6, Normal 0.72, Brisk 0.85; that is the pace, kept), and the Campaign has one pace (Normal): the P key and the pace setting are gone.
- **Pilots ask for orders.** Timed radio calls with three choices, real costs and effects. The call is a small amber list low in the middle: `Up` `Down` pick, `Enter` answers, and the highlight starts on the pilot's own cautious pick (what he does if you say nothing). It waits, dimmed, while a `Q` or `P` list or the map is open. **A press of `Up` or `Down` stops the pilot's 8-second timer** (the bar stays where it was and turns pale blue, the key line says "Timer stopped"), and the call waits for `Enter`; untouched it runs out to the cautious pick as before. No other key stops it. A held call gives up after 30 s (`CAMP_CALLS.held`: the pilot takes the cautious pick and says so), so it cannot block the next one for ever. (Card `space-game-call-timer-stops`, Troy, 22 Nov 2026.)
- **Convoy orders.** Tuck in, Hold here, Run for it, for the ships you escort; the cruiser's shield can cover a ship tucked in.
- **Scout ahead.** An escort route can fork: send a scout, then pick the short dangerous lane or the long quiet one.
- **Enemy torpedo boats** (slow, ignore the fighters, launch a heavy torpedo at the convoy; the turrets and missiles can shoot torpedoes down) and a **rival ship** (the pirate leader: an enemy your size that duels the cruiser in phases, so power management matters).
- **Fighter repair.** A hurt fighter flies home, is mended and relaunches.
- **Ship systems.** Power pips to weapons, shields and engines (keys J K L, `;` to reset); three Long Haul-style gauges (shields, speed, lasers) with power bars; **shields now mend by themselves**, at a rate the shield pips set (the old pay-ore recharge is gone).
- **The briefing of mission 1.1 is the map on every page, with your ships walking the plan** (card `space-game-briefing-map-walk`, Troy, 22 Nov 2026: "the mission map should be used for each page of the briefing, zoomed as needed, with my own craft walking through the plan"). Opening 1.1's briefing from the board shows the map under the heading on all three pages; the cruiser, the wing and the transport walk the road from Luna to Ceres and stop at six points (leave Luna with the patrol, the patrol turns back, a distress call may come, pirates are likely to ambush the road, the pirate leader waits near Ceres, arrive), the map zooms to each, and one line of words sits under it. A page plays by itself and waits at its last stop; `Enter` or `Right` goes on, `Left` back, `Enter` at the very end or `L` launches, `Esc` closes, `+` `-` `Backspace` and the wheel zoom by hand. Dangers are dashed hint rings ("PIRATES?"), never a list of ships. The story, the goals and bonus, and the keys and readiness are small strips under the map (one per page). The other eight missions keep the three-screen briefing; a mission gets a walk by a row of data (`src/data_briefwalk.js`) and its lines. `M` does nothing on a walking map.
- **The tuning panel** (card `space-game-tuning-panel`, Troy, 22 Nov 2026: the way he iterates on feel from now on). `N`, in flight and in the ready room, opens a list of numbers and on/off switches for whole systems; the battle waits while it is open. `Up` `Down` choose, `Left` `Right` change (`Shift` ten steps), `Backspace` puts a row back, `Esc` closes. Each row says when its change is felt (at once, next mission, next call). The changes are kept in this browser (localStorage), a **TUNED** mark shows while any is on, and a mission flown tuned keeps no score and no rank (the level still moves on). **Copy my settings** puts only the changed rows on the clipboard; `node tools/apply-tuning.mjs` reads that block and prints the `CAMP` changes it means (`--write` bakes them in as the new defaults). Bots, node tests and boot scripts never see overrides: only a scene reads the file, and a driven browser (`navigator.webdriver`) neither reads nor writes it. The rows are one table, `CAMP_TUNE` in `src/camptune.js`; the switches are `CAMP.flags`, read through `campFlag`. **First cut on main:** five numbers (cruiser top speed, shield mend rate, turret damage, ore per rock, the pilot call timer) and one switch (the pilots' calls); the rest of the card's numbers and switches follow as further hand-ins.
- **Quality of life.** Zoom out to the whole system (Tab map) and, in flight, out to where a mission's whole route fits on the screen (1.1's is 16,500 px: its farthest zoom is 0.021 and the names of the places and the convoy are drawn as marks there; a short mission's is 0.11); the ore store holds 500; the title starts a mode only on a deliberate choice (Enter, K, arrows then Enter, or a click), not on any key.
