# Campaign: Earth's fleet against the aliens (the first cut)

Card `space-game-campaign-mvp`. A new mode beside Long Haul: the title screen offers both as two panels, a click each: **Long Haul** (Enter, or any other key, as it always was) and the **Campaign** (**K**); `?campaign=N` in the address still opens a level at once. The pause menu of either game goes back to the title: Long Haul's has a **Title screen** row (it saves first, as the pause menu always does; New game stays last) and the Campaign's pause card has **T**; from the title either game starts again (card `space-game-title-modes`, words in `content/text/title.md`). Long Haul, its trading and its save are not touched. The aim is that Troy plays all three levels fast, a few minutes each, and feels the story.

**Status (20 Nov 2026).** Level 1 is now the asteroid belt (card `space-game-campaign-belt`, Troy's new story): the chain is the belt, the Earth refit, Alpha Centauri, the home world, an ending. The Earth defence level (Earth's shield, the Mars and Eros convoys) is out of the chain; its rules still work and it is kept as data under `CAMP.levels.home` for the tests and for a later campaign. The rules are tested in Node (`tests/campaign.mjs`; a plain bot, `tools/campaign-bot.mjs`, wins level 1 in about four to four and a half minutes, a passive player loses). The look, the radar and the feel are seen only by whoever boots it in Chrome. Every number is a line of the `CAMP` table at the top of `src/campaign.js`, and a level is a row of it.

**The story.** The cruiser is a mining ship working the asteroid belt at Ceres, out of the Occator Works. Its fighters are its mining drones, its eight turrets are mining lasers. A quiet minute of mining, then an alien fleet ambushes the belt out of the dark. The mining tools are turned to weapons (the story says so; the keys stay: Space, F, 2, and the ore keys 6 to 9). Three waves and a mothership later the belt is clear; the cruiser flies home, Earth refits it with one of three alien techs (and the ore it mined buys extra ships), and it warps to Alpha Centauri, then to the alien home world. All the story lines are curt (one short sentence, about 12 words at most, fact first: Troy's rule), shown and spoken.

## Missions (card `space-game-campaign-missions`; Troy: "steal from X-Wing: recon, escort, attack, defend")

Each level is a run of three short missions, each with a **briefing** (one screen: its title, the objectives in plain words, the bonus objectives), the **flight**, and a **debrief** (the score, the bonus objectives met, time, hull, ships lost, and the rank when the ranks card is there). A mission lost is flown again (the level is not lost); a mission can be restarted from the pause card (R); after the last mission of a level come the level's story and, in the belt, the Earth refit. The mission table is data, apart from the code that runs it, so a campaign can be made again and again.

**The table.** `CAMP.levels[n].missions` is a list of rows. A row is laid over its level's row (`campMissionRow(level, i)`, which a battle reads as `C.L`), so it may set anything the level sets (`waves`, `convoys`, `rocks`, `guards`, `towers`, `caches`, `relic`, `start`, `startAt`, `defend` ...) and these fields of its own:

| field | what it is |
|---|---|
| `id`, `kind` | its name, and its kind: `recon`, `escort`, `attack`, `defend`, `waves` (the kind is for the hub and the words; what runs is the objectives) |
| `objectives` | what the mission asks; ALL must be met: `scan` (points `{ at, r, secs }`: any ship of yours within r of each for secs), `deliver` (`n` haulers arrive and none is still on its way), `kill` (`what`: `mother`, `towers`, `guards`, `wave:<i>`), `hold` (`secs`: the defended place stands that long), `relic`, `caches` (`n`), `clear`, `ore` (`n`: the store held that much at some time), `cashin` (`n` ore sold at a market outpost) |
| `bonus` | bonus objectives, each worth `CAMP.score.bonus`: `time` (`secs`), `lost` (`n` ships at most), `hull` (`pct` at least), `ore` (`n`), `sold` (`n` ore sold at a market), `delivered` (every hauler) |
| `par` | the seconds it should take (else `CAMP.score.par`): each second under it is worth `CAMP.score.speed` |
| `waves[].on` | a wave that waits for an event instead of a time: `{ scanned: k }`, `{ delivered: k }`, `{ t: s }` (the ambush that hits mid-survey), `{ lane: f }` (a convoy has flown that share of its road; `ahead`: the wave comes from that many px in front of the convoy) |
| `convoys[]` | haulers `{ id, name, say, at, from, to, speed? }`: `from` and `to` are places of the level, `name` its words ("Hauler 1") |
| `defend` | `{ place, shield, name }`: a place with a shield the aliens go for (an Earth-like thing, `C.earth`); lost, the reason is `base` |

A level row with no `missions` is one mission of its own (kind `waves`, no objectives: won by the old rule), which is how the Earth level `home` and the tests' old rows still work. The words: `camp.m.<level>.<mission>.title`, `.b1`, `.b2` (two curt lines), the objective lines `camp.o.*`, the bonus lines `camp.b.*`, the debrief `camp.ui.*`: all in `content/text/campaign-missions.md`.

**The state** (`campProgressLoad` / `campProgressSave`, one JSON under `longhaul.campaign`): `level` and `mission` (the next to fly), `techs`, `ore` and `bonus` (carried into the next mission or level), and `results`: `{ '<level>.<mission>': { score, bonuses: [ids], t, hull, lost, tries } }`, the best score of each mission flown. The API the hub, the ranks and the chatter build on: `campMissions(level)`, `campMissionRow(level, i)`, `campResultKey(level, i)`, `campMissionDone(P, level, i, score)` (keeps the result, moves on, answers `{ levelDone }`), `campMissionTried`, and in a battle `campObjectives(C)` (each objective's state), `campBonuses(C)`, `campScore(C)` (`{ score, base, bonuses, bonus, hull, speed, lost, t, hullLeft, shipsLost }`, also `C.score` when won). The ranks card hangs a rank on the score with a global `campRankLine(P, score)`, which the debrief calls when it exists. A start can ask for a mission: `campNew(level, techs, { mission })`, the scene `go(this, 'Campaign', { level, mission })`; only a level's first mission warps (src/campwarp.js).

**Mission places** (card `space-game-campaign-mission-places`; Troy: "have reasons for needing to use most of the solar system"). A mission row has a `place` (a body of its level's places: the mission happens there), a `dock` (the body whose outpost the debrief returns you to; the place's `outpost` name, from `CAMP.moons` or the level's `outposts`), and its points (`rocks`, `caches`, `guards`, scan points, `waves[].from`/`mother`) are **offsets from the place**, made absolute in `campMissionRow`. Towers use `place` of their own (`towers[].place`), convoys `from`/`to` bodies.

- **The bodies.** The Sol pack gives level 1 the planets (far, drawn when you come near; `src/campsol.js`). `CAMP.moons` adds the moons a level lists in `moons: [ids]`: Luna, Phobos, Deimos, Io, Europa, Ganymede, Callisto, Titan, Enceladus (level 1) and Ran, Kolga, Tyr (level 3); each is `{ id, of (its planet), name, at (px from it), kind, seed, r, outpost }`. A moon is added once its planet is there, and only when the level lists it.
- **The spread.** Level 1: Ceres (the cruiser starts there: no trip), Mars and its moons (the haulers), Europa of Jupiter (the mothership). Level 2: Alpha Centauri B's world, Proxima d, Proxima b; dock at Proxima w. Level 3: Kolga, Ran, the alien world; dock at Tyr. Every level uses at least four bodies (places, docks and where the haulers go; `tests/campaign.mjs` section Q).
- **The trip.** The cruiser starts every mission at its level's start; when that is further than `CAMP.mission.arrive` (3,000 px) from the mission's place, the mission has a trip. The objective line says "Fly to Mars: 2,840 km." (a km is 10 px); the map marks the place as the objective (a gold ring; the view takes it in) and the nav goal is the place, so click-to-fly or Fast takes you there on the map's own rule (`campNavBurn`: twice the speed, 3.2 times beyond 8,000 px, with the wing aboard). `campTravel(level, i)` is the one place that says the distance and the seconds, for the briefing: `PLACE  Mars     2840 km     about 46 s by Fast`.
- **The clock.** Nothing in the mission begins before the cruiser is within 3,000 px of the place: no wave, no hauler, no par time, no hold. From then on `campMissionClock(C)` counts (waves, convoys, the swift bonus, the score's time all read it). The arrival says "Arrived at Mars." and lets the autopilot go. Launch the wing after arriving; a Fast trip leaves it behind.
- **The debrief** says "Docked at Arsia High, Phobos." (`camp.ui.outpost`).
- **No pack.** If a place is not there (the Sol pack is removed) the mission happens where the cruiser starts and its haulers go between the first two places: nothing throws.
- **For the next cards.** The hub, outposts and anomalies read `place`, `dock` and `CAMP.moons`; the anomalies card spreads them over the same bodies.

**The score.** `CAMP.score`: 1,000 for the objectives, 250 for each bonus objective, up to 300 for the hull left, 5 a second under the par, minus 50 for each ship lost.

**The nine missions.** The belt: 1 *Belt survey* (recon: scan three rock fields; the ambush hits when the second is scanned and must be beaten), 2 *Ore haulers* (escort: three haulers fly from Deimos to Phobos, at Mars; two must arrive, the raiders go for them), 3 *The mothership* (attack: three waves, the last with the mothership, at Europa). Alpha Centauri: 1 *Resources* (recon: two of three caches, and the attackers who followed), 2 *The guards* (attack: seven guards round the relic), 3 *The relic* (defend: hold its ring 14 s with no alien near it). The home world: 1 *Approach* (recon: three points on the way in; the defenders come on the first), 2 *Outer ring* (attack: four towers), 3 *Inner ring* (attack: eight towers). A plain bot (`node tools/campaign-bot.mjs`, which flies the missions one by one, `--mission N` for one) wins each in 45 to 75 seconds of fight (the trip to a far place is on top: 46 s to Mars, 37 s to Europa by Fast); a person takes longer; a player who does nothing never wins one (the survey and the approach are never finished, the others are lost).

## Ranks (card `space-game-campaign-ranks`)

You are promoted as you fly. Six ranks, plain names: **Cadet, Ensign, Lieutenant, Commander, Captain, Commodore**. The points are the **best scores of the missions flown, added up** (a mission score already counts its objectives, its bonus objectives, the hull left and the time; a mission flown again for a better score can only raise the total). Nothing is saved for the rank: it is read from the saved results.

| Rank | From points | Extra fighters | Extra escorts | About |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cadet | 0 | 0 | 0 | the start |
| Ensign | 1,000 | 1 | 0 | one good mission |
| Lieutenant | 3,500 | 2 | 1 | about three missions |
| Commander | 6,500 | 3 | 1 | about five |
| Captain | 10,000 | 4 | 2 | about seven |
| Commodore | 14,000 | 5 | 3 | the nine missions flown well |

The effect is on the wing you command: the extras are added to the **bay** at the start of every mission (on top of the bay of six fighters and two escorts, the second-bay tech and the extras of the Earth refit) and to the **wing limit** of the workshop (14 fighters and 6 escorts, plus the extras), so the orders (launch, attack, escort, guard, recall) matter more as you rise. The debrief says the promotion when a mission's score takes you up (`PROMOTED: Lieutenant. The bay holds 8 fighters and 3 escorts.` and a line of the rank's own), and the rank and what the next needs otherwise; the hub (the ready room) shows the rank through `campRankStatus(P)`: `{ name, n, of, points, next, nextName, wing: { fighters, escorts } }`.

The ranks are data: `CAMP.ranks` in `src/campaign.js` (the points and the extras of each), the code is `src/campranks.js`, the words are `content/text/campaign-ranks.md`. A battle is made with `campNew(level, techs, { rank })` (the scene passes the rank of the saved results; no rank is a cadet, so every old battle of a test is as it was). **Balance**: `node tools/campaign-bot.mjs --rank 0|1..5|auto` flies the missions at a rank (auto: 1,400 points a mission flown before it). The missions were already won by every bot at the lowest rank, so the ranks do not change who wins; at the top rank the wing takes about half the damage on the hard missions (the home world's second and third: 60 to 29 and 30 to 18 on average) and the missions end 5 to 10 percent sooner. A battle with no action still loses at every rank (`tests/campranks.mjs`).

**Left for later (the idea bench, `space-game-idea-bench`): a second carrier at a high rank.** Not built; the table has no room for one yet on purpose.

## The ready room: the hub between missions (card `space-game-campaign-hub`; Troy: "I do like how the pilots start out in the space station")

The Campaign starts every mission from a station, like X-Wing's pilots: the **ready room** (scene `CampHub`, `src/camphubscene.js`; the model is `src/camphub.js`, pure, `tests/camphub.mjs`). The title's Campaign choice (K) opens it, and so does every debrief (Enter) and the end of every level (after the story and the refit or pick). It is Long Haul's own station hall (`src/interior.js`, the same baked floor, lights, hologram desks, repair arm and hangar), in the colour of the level's station: the belt outpost (Occator Works), the warp station at Alpha Centauri (Proxima Gate), the last outpost before the home world (Aegir Watch). Nobody walks: the pilot stands at the desk of the row that is chosen, and the cruiser stands on the pad.

Five rows, kept in view at the left of a glass panel; the chosen row's page is at the right; an amber line under the rows says what Enter does.

| key | row | the page |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | MISSIONS | the board: every level and its three missions, flown ones with their score and how many bonus objectives were met, the next one marked NEXT |
| 2 | BRIEFING | the next mission: its title, its kind, the words, the objectives in plain words, the bonus objectives, the best score so far |
| 3 | REFIT | the ore, the tech taken, the wing the next mission starts with; **F** buys a fighter and **E** an escort (the workshop's prices, `CAMP.build`, at most `CAMP.refit.most` of each), **Backspace** gives the last one back; every purchase is saved at once |
| 4 | RANK | the rank and the score, from the ranks card's `campRankStatus(P)` (`src/campranks.js`) when it is there; the plain score and wing when it is not |
| 5 | LAUNCH | the next mission and what happens; **Enter** here, or **L** anywhere, launches |

The keys follow the Campaign's panels (a digit picks, the rows stay in view): **W S** or the arrows move, **Enter** goes on to the next row (and launches on the last), **Esc** or **T** leaves for the title (nothing is lost: the state is saved at every debrief and every purchase). A click on a row picks it; a second click on LAUNCH launches. Launching rolls the hangar door up and starts the Campaign scene on the mission with `via: 'hub'`: the briefing was read in the room, so the scene shows only the level's story (at a level's first mission, after the warp cinematic where there is one) and flies. A lost mission is still flown again from its card (`via: 'retry'`), not through the room.

**The room is data, so a dock can open it** (card `space-game-campaign-outposts`: building ships and repairing the hull happen docked at a friendly outpost, and docking opens this room). A row is its id and: its words (`camp.hub.row.<id>`, `camp.hub.foot.<id>`, in `content/text/camphub.md` and `UI_TEXT`), a note (`CAMP_HUB.note[id](P)`), what Enter does on it (`CAMP_HUB.act[id]`: an action name, or nothing to go on to the next row), its own keys (`CAMP_HUB.rowKey[id](H, P, code, res)`), and in the scene its page (`CAMP_HUBS.page[id](scene)`, drawn with `scene.line(i, col, text, color)`) and where the pilot stands (`CAMP_HUBS.stops[id]`, `.face[id]`); an action's doing is `CAMP_HUBS.run[action](scene)`. The scene is started with `{ rows: [ids], at: id, station: { name, accent, art }, onLaunch(scene), onLeave(scene) }`, all optional: `rows` is the list of rows of this visit (a dock might open `['build', 'repair', 'refit', 'board']`), `onLaunch` replaces "start the next mission" (a dock's way back to the flight it came from), `onLeave` replaces "the title". The refit's F, E and Backspace work in any room that has the `refit` row. `tests/camphubscene.mjs` (section E2) opens a room with a registered row as the example.

**Replaying a mission** (card `space-game-campaign-replay`; Troy: "it would be nice to be able to replay the missions completed"). On the board, every mission with a result shows its best score, the bonus objectives found of all and (with the ranks) the rank its best run gave. **A and D** (or the arrows) move a `>` over the missions done, **R** picks the one under it (`[R]`), **X** goes back to the next mission. With a replay picked the Briefing shows its briefing again (headed REPLAY), the Launch row says `replay 2`, and **Launch** flies it from its standard start, **with your current wing, rank, techs, extras and ore**. The rules: the **story and progress do not move** (the level and the mission to fly next stay, no story card, no warp, no refit, no tech pick at the end: the debrief goes back to the ready room); the result **keeps the best score** and the **bonus objectives found are every one any run met**; the ranks' points are the sum of the best scores, so **a replay raises them only by what it beat the old best by** (flying the same score again farms nothing); the ore comes with the cruiser as ever; one-time rewards are not given twice (the level-end pick and refit are never part of a replay, and a looted anomaly cache is already off the map); **losing a replay costs nothing** (the result is untouched) and R at the pause or Enter at the lose card flies it again as a replay. The debrief is titled REPLAY and says `this run 1,430, your best was 640. A new best: 1,430 points.` (or `Your best stands at 640 points.`). A mission of a level you have not finished can be replayed once it is done. The data is the mission's result in the save (`longhaul.campaign` results: `score, bonuses, t, hull, lost, tries` and now `replays` and `rank`; the top-level keys are unchanged). H in the room opens the page of the room's keys (the READY ROOM group of the Controls table).

**The big briefing** (card `space-game-campaign-briefing`; Troy: "the briefing could come up automatically after selecting a mission, and it can take up more space: have the solar system map, the goals, a walk-through"; then, on his play, card `space-game-briefing-walkthrough`: "zoom in and out, label it Mission 1, three screens to walk through with pictures"). **Enter** or **R** on the board picks the mission under the cursor and opens its briefing **at once** (a mission done is the replay; the next mission is the road), as a screen over most of the window, headed **MISSION 1** and its title (not "1 of 3": which of how many is the board's business), with the names of its three screens under the heading, the one you are on lit. **Enter** or **Right** goes on, **Left** goes back, **Enter** on the last screen launches, **L** launches from any, **Esc** is back to the board. Every other key is the screen's: nothing under it moves. It does not open in an outpost's dock room (nothing there launches a mission). The ship reads the **first walkthrough step once** when the briefing opens, in its voice and by the voice's own settings (muted, off or no voice is silent; no new setting): that line is the voice pack's source `campaign.walk`.
1. **WHERE AND WHY.** At the left the system map (the Tab map's own pieces: the grid, the rings round the star, the belts as rocks, the places and the outposts, `src/campmapdraw.js`' helpers) with the mission's place (the row's `place`, the one the HUD's "Fly to <place>" names) marked in amber, **YOU** where the carrier starts, the dotted route between them and the objective points: scan points as rings (numbered), the haulers' lane as an arrow, the resource pods, the guards, the defence towers, the relic's ring, where the mothership comes in. At the right the place, the **route** said once (one distance: `campTravel` in `src/campaign.js`, with `campKmText`, to the centre of the place, the same number the HUD says; the flight on regular speed and on Fast; a mission that starts at its place has none) and the **story line** (the level's own lines before its first mission, then the mission's two). **+**, **-** and **0** zoom the map as they zoom the Tab map (the `=` and numpad keys too, a held key repeats, a press is the same step, 0.5 to 8 times, 0 resets) and so does the **mouse wheel** over the briefing; the zoom is about the mission's place, which stays where it is on the glass (`campBriefView`, which is `campMapView` of the Tab map), and its percentage reads in the corner of the map as the Tab map's does. **M** goes round three views: the **route** (it opens on this: the start, the place and the points), the **mission area** (the place and its points, close) and the **whole system**, skipping a view that looks the same as the one it leaves, and puts the zoom back to 100% (M does not also mute the sound while the briefing is up: `src/meta.js`).
2. **WHAT TO DO.** The **goals as steps**, numbered, each with a small **picture** made of the game's own sprites (`campBriefPicture`: a fighter in a scan ring, raiders and a drone in a reticle, the haulers on their way to a place, pods, the Reaver for the mothership, the gunship tinted for a tower, the carrier in the relic's ring), then the **bonus objectives** with their points and a smaller picture each (a clock, the wing in a green ring, the cruiser mining a rock, the cruiser and its hull bar, the haulers, the cloaked pod): the words are the HUD's own (`campHubBrief`: the goals and bonus lines of the Briefing row), so the picture is of what the line says.
3. **HOW.** The **walkthrough** of three to six curt steps, the **keys this mission needs** as key caps with what they do (`campBriefKeys`: the basics for every mission, in the order they are used: W A S D, Space, Q 1 to launch the wing, Tab; then what its goals and bonus ask for: Q 3 for haulers, F for a mothership, guards or towers, E 1 for the ore bonus, C 5 for a hull bonus; the zoom last; at most eight), the **enemies** to expect (what the waves, guards and towers add up to) and your **readiness** (the bay, the rank, the stance, the missiles, the ore, the power setting saved in this browser and the shield with its cap, with a warning when the stance is Hold).
- **The walkthrough is data.** The lines are `camp.bf.<level>.<mission>.w<n>` in `content/text/campbrief.md` (no more than twelve words each, no spoilers: nothing of the cloaked caches or the reveal), fixed lines the voice pack records; a mission without them shows one plain line and says nothing. `tests/campbrief.mjs` checks that the numbers in each line (scan points and seconds, haulers and the gap, pods, guards, the hold, towers, when the waves come) are the ones in the mission's data, so a change to the data without the words fails there.
- **Built as two halves.** `src/campbrief.js` is the pure model (layers, bounds, route, the zoom's view, the screens' keys, the goals' pictures, the keys a mission needs, the story, the walkthrough, enemies, readiness; plain Node), `src/campbriefdraw.js` draws it and takes the keys, `src/camphub.js` / `src/camphubscene.js` open it (the board's cursor now stands on the missions done **and the next one**, starting on the next). `tests/campbrief.mjs` runs all of it in Node on a recording stand-in (`tests/briefstand.mjs`); `tests/campbrief-boot.mjs` is the real game with one picture.
- **Readiness.** The wing is always in the bay and the shield full in the ready room, so those are lines, never warnings; the power setting (card `space-game-campaign-systems`) is a line too.

**Not built:** a ready room for the Earth level `home`.

## Outposts: dock to build and mend (card `space-game-campaign-outposts`; Troy: a reason to retreat and regroup across the solar system)

**The rule.** Fighters and escorts can only be **built**, and the hull **mended**, **docked** at a friendly outpost. The shield is recharged in the ship, anywhere, with ore; mining works anywhere. Away from an outpost the build rows are greyed with the reason "Dock at an outpost to build." (the SHIP panel rows 3 and 4, the strip's "build fighters", the voice: one order, `campBuild`, says it).

**A level with no outposts builds and repairs aboard** (wrap-up fix, card `space-game-campaign-no-outpost-rule`; Troy's feature freeze: the supply ship that was to solve it is benched). The rule is the level's own outpost list: a level whose data lists **none** (the alien home world) has nowhere to dock, so the Build and Repair rows work aboard the carrier, **at the same ore costs**, no reason is greyed and the retreat line is never said (`campAboard(C)` in `src/campoutpost.js`). Sol (every level that lists outposts) is unchanged; **Alpha Centauri lists one, the gate at the relic, and keeps the rule** (away from the gate building says "Dock at an outpost to build."). The Fabricator does what it did.

**Outposts are data.** `CAMP.levels[n].outposts = { <place id>: { name?, range? } }`: the Earth level has Earth, the Lunar base and the Mars base; the belt Occator Works (Ceres), Vesta Depot, Earth and the Mars base; Alpha Centauri Proxima Gate (at Proxima b) and the Alpha B relay (at Alpha Centauri B, added by card `space-game-outpost-hot-gate`: the guards of mission 2 sit round Proxima b, so while they live the gate's doors are shut and the relay is the retreat); the home world none (the only place is the enemy's: fight on, or build with the Fabricator). The map marks each with a green frame and a beacon, and the nav list flags it (`outpost: true`). The numbers are `CAMP.outpost` (`range` 280 px from the edge of the place to dock, `clear` 900 px without an alien near, `repairOre` 60 for a whole hull, `retreatHull` 0.35, `fabricator` 120 ore). Fast travel and pursuit (3-GEN) use the same list: `campOutposts(level)`, `campOutpostNear(C)`, `campOutpostNearest(C)` in `src/campoutpost.js`.

**Docking.** In range of an outpost, **E**, the SHIP panel's sixth row (key 2 then 6) or "dock at Luna" (typed or spoken) docks: the carrier stops, the battle is paused and **the ready room opens as that outpost** (`src/campdock.js` registers the rows; `src/camphubscene.js` is the room): BUILD (F a fighter, E an escort, through the order), REPAIR (R mends the hull for ore in proportion to what is missing), REFIT (the tech, and **U fits the Fabricator**), MISSIONS (the board), UNDOCK (Enter on it, or L; Esc and T too). Aliens within 900 px keep the doors shut ("Aliens too close to dock at Lunar base, pilot."), so docking is not a free breather in a fight. Builds are queued as ever and join the bay after their time, in flight.

**The Fabricator** (the tech `fabricator`, the flag `campHas(C, 'fabricator')`) lets the ship build fighters and escorts anywhere; hull repair stays docked. **My call: it is an item at a refit, not a pick after a level or a rank reward**: 120 ore (the store holds 200, so it is reachable by mining in the belt) at the dock's REFIT row or the REFIT row between missions, bought once, kept in the progress. Not given at the start.

**The retreat.** Hull below 35 percent and no ship of the wing left (in the bay, out, launching or building): the HUD line under the goal says **"Retreat to Luna: 12 s on Fast."** (the nearest outpost by the edge of its place, the flight the map would say on Fast), the ship says it once, and **the goal of the map and the edge arrow is that outpost** (the Fast button flies there). In range it says "E  dock at Luna" instead. Typing "retreat to Luna" flies there; a bare "retreat" is still the wing's.

## How you play

You fly the carrier yourself: big, slow, heavy. **W A S D** fly it, hold **Space** to make its eight turrets fire at the ships close by, **F** fires a homing missile, and **B** blinks (once you have that tech; it was Q until Q and E opened the panels). There is no other gun (see The weapons below). **Esc** pauses, with its own menu. Your small ships do the rest, and you give them orders by **keys**, by the **command strip** (I, then type) or by **voice** (U, then say it). The three ways run the same five orders:

| Order | Key | You can type or say | What the small ships do |
|---|---|---|---|
| Launch | Q then 1 | "launch", "launch fighters" | the bay opens: one ship out every 0.6 s, six fighters and two escorts |
| Attack | Q then 2 | "attack", "attack the mothership" | fighters go for the nearest alien, or the mothership if you name it |
| Escort | Q then 3 | "send escorts to the Mars convoy" | escorts fly beside the convoy you name (or the nearest one) and shoot what comes at it; in a level with no convoys (the belt) they stay close to the cruiser |
| Guard | Q then 4 | "guard the carrier", "defend Earth" | the wing circles what you name |
| Recall | Q then 5 | "recall", "come home" | they fly back, land in the bay and are repaired; a ship lost stays lost |

**Every mark of the map is a target (card `space-game-campaign-map-targets`, Troy, 20 Nov 2026).** A click on ANY mark of the Tab map sets it, as a click on a place did: your own ships (each one of the wing, and "Your ships"), the convoys, every alien ("Raider 3", "Gunship 2", "Drone 5"), the mothership and the towers, the ore and wrecks lying about, the rocks ("Rock 4"), the caches, scan points and relic, and the places (planets, outposts). Marks for the rocks (grey) and the ore (gold) are drawn for it. The set mark has its ring, the line from the carrier, its distance and the two buttons, Fly and Fast, with Enter and Shift+Enter; the wing-aboard rule and the pursuit rules are as before. The nav list is the same rows by keys: goal, places, convoys, enemies, caches and relic, ore and rocks, your ships, nearest first in each (`campNavRows`, src/campnav.js). **The pick** (`campNavPickStack`): the nearest mark to the cursor within `CAMP.nav.pick` px; two marks on one spot are a stack, and a second click on the same spot (within `stackSame` px) takes the next one of it (a lone mark flies on the second click, as it did). **A moving target is led and followed** (`campNavLead`, `campNavDrive`): the carrier aims where the ship will be, closes at the Long Haul autopilot's rule in the target's frame (a ship faster than the carrier is chased at its top speed), and at the **stand-off range** keeps station with its speed matched, following until the trip is ended: `CAMP.nav.stopEnemy` 400 px from an alien (turret range), `stopFriend` 130 alongside your ship or a convoy, `stopRock` 200 from a rock (inside the mining laser), a point 40 (ore: pickup range), the mothership and a tower `stopShip` 450 as before. A target that dies (or a rock that breaks) ends the trip: "Autopilot off: Raider 3 is gone."; ore that is taken is "arrived". When the map is opened during a trip its place is set again with the buttons and the active one marked. No attack orders here (commanding the wing from the map is the next card). The names are in content/text/campmaptargets.md.

**Fast needs the wing aboard (card `space-game-campaign-fast-board`, Troy, 20 Nov 2026).** The burn (the map's Fast button, Shift+Enter, "fly fast to Mars", Shift and 5 on the SHIP panel) is `CAMP.nav.fast` = 2.2 times the carrier's speed (462 px/s, 672 on a far trip), above a fighter's 430 and an escort's 300, so the small ships could not keep up: **the wing has to be aboard before the carrier burns**. With a ship out (or still in the launch queue) the first press is refused in words ("Wing is out: recall it first. Press again to recall and burn."); a second press within `CAMP.nav.askFor` (8 s) recalls the wing and burns by itself as soon as the last ship is aboard, the status line counting them off ("Recalling... 3 out"); if a ship cannot get back within `CAMP.nav.recallWait` (20 s) the burn is given up and the ships still out are named ("Still out: fighter 3, escort 1"). A steering key, X or a regular trip gives up a burn that is waiting; launching the wing during a burn drops it to regular speed. **Regular Fly stays allowed with the wing out** (they keep up). The rule is in one door, `campNavFly` (src/campnav.js), so the map, the words and the panel all obey it, and `campNavGo` cannot be made to burn with a ship out. The edge arrow has no action, so it has nothing to obey. **Mid-flight (Troy, 20 Nov 2026): the two map buttons, Enter and Shift+Enter stay live for the whole trip.** A place you fly to stays set with its Fly and Fast buttons beside it; the one that is on is marked ON; a press on the other changes the speed of the same trip (`campNavGo` keeps the trip object and its start when the place is the same, so nothing is re-planned), and the wing-aboard rule is asked of the switch to Fast like of any burn (with a ship out the carrier stops while the wing comes home, because a ship cannot land on a carrier that is flying away, and the burn takes the same trip over when the last is aboard; a wait that times out resumes the regular trip). The set place and its buttons go when the trip ends (arrived, X, a steering key). Long Haul's sector map does the same by being opened again mid-flight (its destination is kept, its buttons are live, Fly or Fast is marked ON, Shift+Enter makes the same trip Fast); a trip already in **cruise** cannot be switched back to regular (the game says "Cruising already. S cancels.").

**Pursuit: a burn is a retreat (card `space-game-campaign-pursuit`, Troy, 20 Nov 2026).** Every alien's top speed is under the burn's 462 px/s (raider 430, drone 260, gunship 150; the mothership 55 and the towers do not chase) and over the carrier's regular 210 for the raider and the drone, so at regular speed they keep up and on Fast they fall behind. An alien that has been on the carrier (it targeted it, within its **leash**: `CAMP.alien.pursuit.leashOf` 1.25 times its firing range, so 650 px for a raider, 810 a drone, 975 a gunship) and then stays beyond the leash for `loseAfter` (1.5 s) **drops the chase** (`campPursuit` in src/campaign.js) and the ship says "Lost them." (once for a pack: `sayEvery` 8 s). A guard goes back to its post and holds there again; in a level with no Earth the rest come to rest where they are and stay out of it until the carrier comes within the leash again; in the Earth level a wave goes on to Earth as it was. One that was never near (it is coming from afar) has nothing to lose and keeps coming; one that is hit, or sees one of your other ships in reach, is on it again. The raider is only 32 px/s under the burn, so a short burn (under about 5 s) does not shake one: a trip to somewhere a few thousand px off does (the far burn, 672, certainly).

**The keyboard panels (cards `space-game-campaign-keys-help` and `space-game-campaign-panels-ui`, Troy, 20 Nov 2026).** The digits are not orders on their own any more. Two panels are always on the screen as narrow collapsed **tabs**, `Q FLEET` at the left edge and `E SHIP` at the right, below the gauges and the radar so nothing is covered. **Q** (or a click on the tab) opens FLEET: its tab lights and its five rows drop under it in a narrow column, one line each: the digit, the name and a word or two of the live state, or greyed with the reason ("all out", "needs 45 ore"); **E** does the same for SHIP at the right (mine ore, recharge, build fighter, build escort, fly to goal). A row's digit does it, so *Q then 3* is "escort": the row **flashes** twice in 160 ms and the panel collapses to its tab; a pick made before the list has dropped in (120 ms) pulses the **tab** once instead. **Q** again closes FLEET, **E** again SHIP, Q with SHIP open switches (the two lists cross-fade), Esc or any other key closes without a pick, and a digit with no panel open does nothing. The tabs show in the battle only (not over a card, the pause, the page of keys or the Tab map). The drawing is one component for both panels, from the table in `src/data_controls.js` (`CAMP_PANELS`: a row, a panel with its key, side and `slot`, and the timings in `anim` are data), so a new panel or row is drawn without a line of code. The bottom key line has a thin backing strip so nothing is drawn through it. Digits with no panel open do nothing, N does nothing in the Campaign (B is the warp blink; the Tab map's list is where the places are), and typing or saying an order is unchanged. **H** opens one page of every key (two columns, the panels first); it is on the pause card and the title's Campaign panel, and the bottom key line ends "Q Fleet   E Ship   Tab Map   H Keys". One table feeds all of it: `CAMP_PANELS` and the CAMPAIGN and PANELS groups of `src/data_controls.js` (the panels, their rows, keys and words), read by `src/camppanel.js` (what a row does and whether it can be done now), `src/campanelsdraw.js` (the drawing), `src/campkeys.js` (the page), the key line, the pause card, the ship computer's rules and the tests; a later card adds a row, or the centre panel, there. The merged train adds: **Shift with 5 on the SHIP panel is the burn** (the wing must be aboard), the SHIP panel's sixth row **docks** at an outpost (so docking is E then 6; the HUD hint says so), and the third panel, **SYSTEMS on C** (the ship's power), is a tab beside FLEET; the warp blink is on **B**.

**Zoom (card `space-game-campaign-zoom`, Troy, 20 Nov 2026).** **+** (the **=** key and numpad **+** too) zooms the view in, **-** (numpad **-**) zooms it out, **0** goes back to the default, and the mouse wheel zooms as it does in the Long Haul (`src/camzoom.js`: 0.4 to 1.6, eased, the HUD, panels and strip stay their size). The zoom is kept for the level (a retry comes back as it was) and starts at the default on the next. With the Tab map open the same keys and the wheel zoom the map, from half to eight times the level fitted to the frame, about the place you set by a click or chose in the list (else the middle); the level is held in the frame, **0** shows all of it, and the zoom level is in the corner. A click on the map, and the arrow at the edge of the screen, are right at every zoom. The numbers are `CAMZOOM` (`src/camzoom.js`) and `CAMPZOOM` (`src/campzoom.js`); the key is one row of the table in `src/data_controls.js`, so the H page lists it.

An order the game cannot do is answered in words and does nothing ("the bay is empty"). The ship's voice answers each one in a short line.

## Stances (card `space-game-campaign-stances`)

A stance is **how the wing moves when it attacks or guards**. One for the whole wing, kept across orders (launch, attack, guard, recall do not change it) and saved with the progress, so it comes with every mission. Five, set by the order `stance` (the strip, the voice, and the FLEET panel's second page: Q, then the Left or Right arrow flips between the orders and the stances, `CAMP_PANELS.pages` is 2; the tab then says STANCES and a hint line under the list says where the arrows go, the stance in force is marked "in force", a digit or a click picks one, and the collapsed FLEET tab names the stance when it is not Free; a panel always opens on its first page):

| Stance | Words | What the wing does |
|---|---|---|
| Free | "free", "fight freely" | the pirates' own steering (circle, pass, break, run): how the wing always flew |
| Orbit | "orbit", "orbit the mothership", "circle" | circle the target at `CAMP.stance.orbit` px (from its edge), the nose on it, firing; half the ships go round each way at `orbitSpeed` |
| Keep distance | "keep distance", "keep your distance from the mothership", "stand off" | stand off at `stand` (0.85) of each ship's own gun range from the target: farther off it comes in, closer it backs away, and it sidesteps back and forth (`weave`, 140 px/s) so it is not a still target; the ships take the towers and the mothership about a fifth faster than free |
| Match speed | "match speed", "in formation" | fly in formation, a ring of `formation` px (200) round the carrier that turns with it, at the carrier's speed, and fire at what comes into range; it is what Escort does with no convoy (Troy asked for the Escort row to be called "Escort / match speed"; the label does not fit the panel's column as drawn today, so it is renamed with the panels' redraw) |
| Hold position | "hold position", "stay put" | stay where they are (brake hard), fire at what comes into range; when the carrier has gone `leash` px (1,400) they come back to it |

Orbit and Keep follow the order's target (the nearest alien, or the one you named; "orbit the mothership" also sends the fighters at it) and are as free when there is none. Match and Hold fight what is in range of the ship whatever the order's target. A stance applies to **Attack and Guard** (and Escort with no convoy, which is Guard at the carrier); Escort to a convoy, Recall and Mine are the same in every stance. The old words are as they were ("guard", "hold", "stay with me" are Guard); only "orbit" moved, from Guard to the stance. All the numbers are `CAMP.stance`; the ids and their order `CAMP.stances`.

**The FLEET panel's second page.** Page 1 is the orders (Launch, Attack, Escort, Guard, Recall); page 2 (`CAMP_PANELS.panels[0].page2`) is the stances as rows 1 to 5 with the one in force marked, flipped with the Left and Right arrows while the panel is open; the collapsed tab says it (`FLEET - ORBIT`, `campPanelTab`). The logic is done and tested (`src/camppanel.js`, `tests/campstances.mjs`); it is switched on by `CAMP_PANELS.pages = 2`, which waits for the panels' redraw (`space-game-campaign-panels-ui`) to draw the second page: until then the stances are the strip's and the voice's, and the H page does not list the stance keys.

## The weapons (card `space-game-campaign-missiles`)

The carrier has two ways to hurt an alien, one key each (card `space-game-campaign-turrets` took away the single main gun):

| Weapon | How | What it is for |
|---|---|---|
| Turrets | hold **Space**: eight turrets spread along the hull (four a side, two near the nose, two at the stern, rows of `CAMP.carrier.mounts`); each aims itself at the nearest alien within 500 px of it (to the edge of the alien), always, and fires one shot of 4 hit points every 0.7 s, but only while Space is held and only at something in reach; nothing fires on its own. The eight do not fire together: each has a slot of its own in the 0.7 s cycle, a turret's width of the cycle (about 90 ms) behind the one before it, moved a little at random (12 ms), so the fire is a continuous rattle and not a volley; a slot with nothing to shoot at, or with Space up, is let go, so nothing bursts when Space is pressed. Each turret leads its shot: it points where its shot and the alien will meet (the alien's speed, the shot's 800 px/s and the carrier's own drift), so a gunship crossing at 100 px/s or a raider at its top speed is hit, not just a tower that stands still | the small ships that come close, and any big one you fly up to; 46 hit points a second in all |
| Missile launcher | **F**; "fire missiles" or "missiles at the mothership" typed on the strip (I) or spoken (U) | big ships: the mothership, the towers and the gunships |

On the screen each turret is a dark base with an amber dot; once it has picked an alien it points a short barrel at it (even with Space up), and when it shoots it flashes white with a longer barrel. A faint ring round the carrier shows how far they reach, and their shots are amber (the beam tech's are cyan). Each shot has a muzzle flash (a short spark at the barrel tip) and a quiet blip whose pitch changes from turret to turret, at most one blip in 70 ms, so eight turrets rattle and do not stack into noise.

A missile is a homing round. It goes for the mothership if the mothership is within 2,400 px, else the nearest tower or gunship within that reach; named ("at the mothership") it goes for the mothership only, and says so if the mothership is not there or too far. It leaves the tube slowly, gets up to speed (560 px/s) and turns at 3.4 rad/s, so one fired the wrong way still comes round. It carries 7 seconds of fuel, then it dies with no bang (a plain puff). Its fuze goes off near a big ship only: a raider or a drone on its way does not stop it. Where it goes off, every alien within 80 px is hurt: a big ship 100 hit points, any other alien 10 (a raider has 20, a drone 30). Six missiles take 600 of the mothership's 700 (see `CAMP.mother.hp`), the guns and the wing finish it; a tower (160) takes two; a gunship (80) one. A smoke trail follows it and the bang is a plain explosion.

The magazine is small and the reload is slow: you start with 6, one comes back every 15 seconds while it is below 6, and the launcher cycles for 0.7 s between two launches. The HUD shows `F  MISSILES  4    next in 9 s` at the bottom left, in red when it is empty. The count is one plain number, `C.missiles`; the ore of the resources card adds to it through `campMissileAdd(C, n)` (up to 12, the most the magazine holds; the slow reload does not fill it past 6). All the numbers are `CAMP.missile` in `src/campaign.js`. The key line and the pause card name F; the ship computer has the order `fleet_missiles` (the model is told the key and the act in a battle).

## The sounds (card `space-game-campaign-sounds`)

All synthesized in `src/audio.js` from the game's own voices (no audio files), through the effects volume of Settings > Sound, the master volume and M, like every effect. `SFX_BUILT` in that file says in words what each is made of; the numbers are in each sound's own line. To ask for a change, use the name.

| Name | When | Made of |
|---|---|---|
| `turret` | each turret shot, at most one in 70 ms | a short bright square bolt falling to under a third of its pitch in 70 ms, a thin sawtooth zip, a hair of click; the pitch is the turret's (1500 Hz plus 150 Hz a step, the back four higher) moved 3 percent at random: a staggered pew-rattle |
| `wingShot` | each shot of a fighter or an escort (the sim's `wingshot` event), at most one in 55 ms | the wing's gun, a quieter, farther pew: a soft triangle bolt falling 1 kHz to 350 Hz in 60 ms, a thin sine zip and a faint tick; softer the farther the ship is from the carrier (1 minus the distance over 1,600 px, none beyond) |
| `beamhum` (a loop, `beamHum`) | the Beam turrets firing; a mining laser on a rock (the belt card's `C.lasers`; a plain turret shot at an alien is also a beam there but keeps its pew, unless `CAMP.beamHum.alienLaser` is 1) | a sine root, a sine fifth and a sawtooth buzz an octave up through a low-pass that opens as it climbs, a thin band of hiss; 140 to 320 Hz over two seconds of unbroken fire |
| `missileLaunch` | F | a whoosh that climbs for half a second (a rising noise band and a rising sawtooth), then at 0.3 s a dull thump |
| `missileBlast` | a missile goes off | a low sine thump, a low-passed rumble, a crackle of five quick bright bursts |
| `missileFizzle` | a missile runs out of fuel | a soft puff of air and a small falling tone |
| `blastSmall` / `blastBig` | a ship dies / the mothership or the carrier dies | a thump and a rumble with a crackle of four / eight bursts; the big one is two thumps and a second long |
| `shieldHit` | a shot on the carrier's shield | a bright electric zap, high and short |
| `hullHit` | a shot that got through to the hull | a dull clank, low and short |
| `earthHit` | a shot on Earth's shield | a deep thump felt more than heard |

**Levels, measured (card `space-game-campaign-turret-sound-bug`).** Troy could not hear the turrets or the wing. `node tests/campsound-probe.mjs` (headless Chrome, one Chrome through the lock) puts an AnalyserNode on the master output and reads the loudest 46 ms of each sound. Before: the eight turrets -40.8 dBFS RMS (5 dB under Long Haul's laser, 24 under a kill), the wing's guns no sound at all (they fired no event), the beam hum -41.4. The Campaign has no music (only the Space scene has a mood), so music did not duck anything; the cause was the level and the missing event. After: turrets -31.3, the wing's pew about -37 up close (a quieter, farther sound by design), the hum -35.4, the shield zap and hull clank -34.5, the missile -25, a kill -29 to -21. The level of each group is one line of `SOUND.level` in `src/audio.js` (1 is as the sound is written); the hum's is `BEAMHUM.vol`; how often each plays and how far the wing's pew carries is `CAMP_SND` in the scene.

The rules they keep (`tests/campsounds.mjs`, plain Node on a stand-in AudioContext): each is short, no louder than the game's own kill, made on the pool's voices (no new audio node however long a fight runs), silent when muted, at volume 0 or with the effects at 0, scaled by the effects volume, with a share of the reverb that leaves the kill, the jump, the arrival and the dock the longest tails. The scene limits how often each kind plays (`CAMP_BLIP`, `CAMP_SND` in `src/campaignscene.js`).

## Anomalies: the cloaked caches (card `space-game-campaign-anomalies`; Troy: "spots where space looks odd and warped, like the Predator's invisibility")

Off the missions' routes there are spots where space looks warped: **cloaked enemy caches**, a store of the aliens' weapons or a lab of their tech. The cloak fools the sensors (no contact, no name, no blip) but not the eye: it shows as an **anomaly**, a patch of heat-haze, and nothing is known about it until you come. The table is `CAMP.levels[n].anomalies` (the belt five: three in the belt, one round Mars and one round Jupiter's moon Io, a Fast trip of under a minute; Alpha Centauri and the home world three each); the numbers are `CAMP.anomaly`; the code is `src/campanomaly.js` (the sim, no Phaser) and `src/campanomalydraw.js` (the haze).

1. **Noticed.** The carrier within `sense` (2,400 px; the Lens tech doubles it) notices it once: the ship says "Odd readings ahead", the radio calls it, and it has a mark on the **radar** (a violet blip that flickers) and on the **Tab map** (broken rings and a question mark, in the legend as Anomalies) and a row in the **nav list** named "Anomaly" with its distance. Nothing says what it hides. The words find it: "fly to the anomaly".
2. **The haze.** In the game view it is a layer of broken rings that ripple outward, a faint lens and the stars behind it pulled and doubled, drawn from `haze` (1,500 px), stronger as the carrier nears; no shader, no asset (the graphics' own calls, alpha at most 0.55). The ring of the visit fills while the carrier holds there.
3. **The visit.** The carrier within `visit` (260 px) for `hold` (2) seconds decloaks it (the count falls back when it leaves). The autopilot stops at `stop` (160 px), inside it, so "Fast" there is enough. The flash, then the cache is drawn (a ring with crates and a dish), the ship says what it is, and its **cloaked guards** come out on a ring `ring` px round it and go for the carrier (the row's `guards`: raiders, drones, gunships).
4. **The loot, once.** The last guard dead, a beat later the reward: **ore** as chunks lying on the cache (`chunk` ore each, any ship picks them up), **a heavier weapon** (more missiles in the magazine, up to `missile.max`: `C.magazine`; a heavier turret: `C.turretMul`, times the turrets' damage), and **a tech item** that the Earth refit offers as one more choice (Overdrive: the carrier a quarter faster; Lens: anomalies noticed twice as far). The loot is saved with the progress (`P.found`: `'<level>.<id>'`) and **does not come back**; the weapons stay with the pilot (each battle begins with the magazine and the turrets of everything looted so far, read from the saved keys).
5. **The refit.** The Earth refit lists what was looted after the three techs, **numbered 6, 7 and 8** (4 and 5 keep buying ships); the plain pick after the later levels lists them as 4 and on. What was not picked is offered again at the next pick.
6. **Bonus objectives.** A mission asks for one of its level's anomalies as a bonus, `{ id: 'find', type: 'anomaly', anomaly: '<id>' }` ("Find the cloaked cache"): met when the cloak is down in that flight, or when it was looted before (it was found). The score, the debrief and the ranks use it like any bonus. Nine missions, nine anomalies; the Mars and Io ones are for the loot alone.

## Wing orders with groups and targets (card `space-game-campaign-wing-targets`; Troy: "command the fleet from the map")

The sim side of the map's orders. An order names a **group**, a **verb** and a **target**: `campWingOrder(C, group, verb, target, how)` in `src/campwing.js` (pure; `tests/campwing.mjs`). The table is `CAMP.wing`.

- **Groups.** Three, as data: `fighters`, `escorts` (the escorts, and the scouts they send out) and `all`. A group is a list of kinds or of ship ids (`{ name, kinds }`, `{ name, ships }`), so numbered groups drop in later as `C.groups[id]` (they are read before the table; nothing builds them yet). An order that names no group is the verb's own, today's: Attack the fighters, Escort the escorts, Guard, Hold, Mine and Recall everyone, Scout and Fetch the escorts.
- **Targets.** An enemy, a friend, a convoy, a place, a point `{ x, y }`, a pickup, a cache, an anomaly (one that is noticed) or a rock: by id (`raider-3`, `freighter-h1`, `carrier`, `rock-4`, `chunk-9`), by a nav row of the map (`place:vesta`, `cache:0`, `anom:veil`, `scan:1`, `mother`, `tower:...`, or the row itself), as a point, or in words ("mars", "the anomaly"). No target is the verb's default (Attack: the nearest alien, Guard: the carrier, Escort: the nearest convoy). `campWingTarget` gives `null` (none), `false` (names nothing now) or `{ kind, id, name, x, y, r }`.
- **The table** (`CAMP.wing.verbs`): attack takes an enemy; escort a friend or a convoy; guard a place, a point, a friend or a convoy; hold a place or a point (fly there, stop, fire at what comes into range: `holdAt`); scout a place, a point or an anomaly (the scouts card's order; refused in words until it is in); fetch ore or a cache (fly over it: a ship of yours takes what it touches); mine a rock or ore; recall none. A pair outside the table is refused in plain words ("Fighters cannot escort an enemy."); so is a target that is gone ("I cannot find that target."), a verb that needs a target, a group that does not exist, no ships in the group (the old lines). A refusal does nothing.
- **The old orders** (Attack, Escort, Guard, Recall; the keys, the panel, the strip, the voice) go through the same entry point with no group and no target: the same answers and the same orders. The stances apply to Attack and Guard, with a target too (ORBIT circles the target the order names).
- **For the map** (`campWingState(C)`): one row for each group that has ships: its order, whether its ships differ (`mixed`), its target (live: a ship moves) with a position, the centre of the group and where its ships are, and the `line` from the centre to the target for the dotted line. `campWingVerbs(C, target)` lists the verbs that take a target (the order menu), `campWingSmart` the one a right-click does (`CAMP.wing.smart`). `campWingCancel(C, group)` (X) drops a group's order: its ships guard the carrier again (Recall sends them home). Every order is an event `{ type: 'wingorder', group, verb, target, n, how }` (`how`: key, strip, voice, click, map).
- **Words.** "fighters at the mothership", "escorts to Mars", "all attack the gunship", "escorts scout the anomaly", "fighters hold at Ceres", "cancel the fighters": the order `wing` of the strip and the voice (the ship computer's act `fleet_wing`); with no verb the target's kind says it (a right-click). The old forms ("fighters attack the mothership", "all guard Ceres", "escorts to the Mars convoy") keep their words and answers.

## Pirates, neutral traffic and cues (card `space-game-l1-pirates-traffic`; Troy, 21 Nov 2026: "let there be other ships in the game")

Level 1 is the rim run (card `space-game-l1-rim-missions`): the world has other ships in it. The pure half is `src/camppirates.js` (`tests/camppirates.mjs`); the numbers are `CAMP.pirate` and `CAMP.traffic`.

- **Pirates** are human raiders: `skiff`, `cutter`, `brute` (they fly and shoot like the raider, the drone and the gunship, with hulls and damage of their own, `CAMP.pirate.kinds`). They are on the hostile side (`team: 'alien'`, so the turrets, the wing, the kill objectives, the map marks and the wrecks need no change) with `u.faction === 'pirate'`; the aliens' is `'alien'`, the cruiser's and the wing's `'earth'`, the neutrals' `'neutral'`. The words never call a pirate an alien (the ship computer is told "Pirates alive: n" apart from the aliens). Yellow on the radar and the map.
- **A wave row names them** in `ships`, as ever: `{ at | on, from, ships: { skiff: 4, cutter: 1 }, goal, say, prey, breaks, breakOn, breakAt }`. `prey`: `'haulers'`, `'miners'` (the neutral miners and your own ships on a Mine order), `'carrier'` or `'any'` (the default). A pirate weighs what it can attack by distance times `CAMP.pirate.prey` (the hauler 0.5, the miner 0.55, your escort 0.9, a fighter 1.1, a patrol 1.3, the cruiser 1.6; the least wins), so a hauler or a miner is wanted over the cruiser. `goal: 'convoy:h1'` still wins over prey; a ship it was hit by pulls it round for `CAMP.pirate.distract`; when the prey the row names is not there it takes what is.
- **Breaking off.** A pirate under `CAMP.pirate.breakHp` of its hull (the row's `breakAt` is its own) runs. So does a whole wave: `breaks: s` (seconds after it came), `breakOn: '<cue>'`, or by hand `campPirateBreak(C, 'all' | 'wave:N')`. A runner flies away from your ships at `CAMP.pirate.run` times its speed, does not shoot, and is **gone** once it is `CAMP.pirate.gone` px from every ship of yours (and at least `runMin` ms have passed): taken out of the battle, not killed (no kill, no wreck), counted in `C.piratesEscaped`; its wave is cleared for `{ type: 'kill', what: 'wave:N' }`. A slow brute can still be caught.
- **Cues** are names a script fires: `campCue(C, 'name')` (once; `C.cues.name` is the clock; a `cue` event). A wave starts on one (`on: { cue: 'name' }`), breaks on one (`breakOn`), and a mission row fires them itself: `cues: [{ name: 'contact', on: { delivered: 2 } }]`, `on` being any condition a wave takes: `scanned`, `delivered`, `t`, `cue`, and the new `cleared: 'wave:0'` (it came, and none of it is left).
- **Neutral traffic** (`team: 'neutral'`) is in `C.ships` but in nobody's wing: not hostile to you, never counted as a ship of yours lost; the pirates and the aliens are at war with it (a hostile shot hurts it, its shot hurts them, yours does not). A level or mission row lists it: `traffic: [{ id, kind: 'patrol' | 'miner', place + off | at: [x, y], r, n, name, home, field }]` (`at` and `field` are offsets from the mission's place). A **patrol** is armed, ranges round its post, shoots a hostile within its range and goes to one within `help` px of its post: a mission without patrol rows has no help (the rim). A **miner** is unarmed: it works the nearest rocks of the mission (it does not cut them: the ore is yours), goes home to its place and docks, goes out again, and runs home when a hostile comes within `fear`. A neutral lost is `C.neutralsLost`, a `neutral` event and a line from the ship; `{ type: 'neutrals', n }` is a bonus for at most n lost.
- **On the map.** Patrols (teal) and miners (pale) are marks, legend rows and a TRAFFIC group in the list (you can fly to one and escort it); a pirate is an enemy row named for its kind ("Skiff 3"); the goal of a kill mission against pirates says "Pirate".
- **Words** are `content/text/campaign-pirates.md`.

## Ship systems: power to weapons, shields and engines (card `space-game-campaign-systems`; Troy, 20 Nov 2026: "manage my ship's resources and systems, energy, shields, engine, like X-Wing")
*Design lines, written before the code. Keep it light.* The carrier has **three power pools**, WEAPONS, SHIELDS and ENGINES, and a **fixed budget of 6 pips** to share between them, 0 to 4 in each. The default is **2 / 2 / 2 and it gives today's numbers exactly** (every effect below is a multiplier of 1 at 2 pips, so the game plays as before until the player moves a pip; the bot, `tools/campaign-bot.mjs`, plays at 2 / 2 / 2 and must still win). Everything is one table, `CAMP.power` in `src/campaign.js`, indexed by pips 0 to 4:
| pips | 0 | 1 | **2** | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| WEAPONS: the turrets' interval (x `pdDelay` 700 ms) | 1.6 | 1.3 | **1** | 0.8 | 0.65 |
| WEAPONS: the missile reload (x 15 s) | 1.6 | 1.3 | **1** | 0.75 | 0.55 |
| SHIELDS: the shield cap (x 150) | 0.5 | 0.75 | **1** | 1.25 | 1.5 |
| SHIELDS: regeneration, points a second (after 3 s with no hit; today there is none) | 0 | 0 | **0** | 3 | 6 |
| ENGINES: top speed, and the Fast burn with it | 0.75 | 0.88 | **1** | 1.12 | 1.25 |
| ENGINES: thrust | 0.7 | 0.85 | **1** | 1.15 | 1.3 |
| ENGINES: turn | 0.8 | 0.9 | **1** | 1.1 | 1.2 |
| ENGINES: the warp blink's recharge (x 20 s) | 1.5 | 1.25 | **1** | 0.8 | 0.6 |
- **Weak, never dead:** zero pips is the first column: the turrets fire at 5 shots in 8, the shield is half and does not mend, the carrier crawls at three quarters. Nothing switches off.
- **The budget rule** (`campPowerShift`): a pip moves, the total stays 6. "Power to shields" takes a pip from the system that has the most (a tie: weapons, then shields, then engines) unless you name one; a system at 4 refuses ("at full power"), and there is nothing to take when the others are at 0. **Balance** is back to 2 / 2 / 2 at once.
- **Emergency shields:** all four pips to shields (weapons 1, engines 1) for 10 seconds, then back to what it was; ready again 40 seconds after it started. While it runs the pips are locked ("emergency shields hold the power"). It does not refill the shield (ore does): it is the cap, and 6 points a second of regeneration, for ten seconds.
- **Why it matters:** shields up before a fight (a bigger shield that mends), engines up for the run to an outpost or a retreat, weapons up against the mothership (the missiles come back in 8 seconds, the turrets rattle faster). At 2 / 2 / 2 nothing changes.
- **Where it shows:** three small pip bars under the gauges, W S E, the pips lit (HUD); the **SYSTEMS** panel on **C**, a collapsed tab like Q FLEET and E SHIP (7-GEN's panels-ui; rows 1 Weapons +, 2 Shields +, 3 Engines +, 4 Balance, 5 Emergency shields, each with its live state); the strip and the voice ("power to shields", "balance power", "emergency shields"); the ship's computer and the wing's radio say a short line when the shields or the engines run low. The setting is kept in the Campaign's save and is how the next level starts (a level start, not a battle: the emergency never carries over).
- **Built (what it is in the code):** `CAMP.power` and the `campPower*` functions in `src/campaign.js` (the three orders are `campOrder(C, 'power', 'shields' | 'shields:weapons')`, `'balance'` and `'emergency'`, so the panel, the strip and the voice are one set of rules); the setting is kept in this browser under `longhaul.campaign.power` (apart from the level progress: it is read when a level starts and written the moment it changes; never the emergency's pips); the HUD pips are three rows of four small squares at the right of the bay line (W amber, S cyan, E green, the shields white in an emergency); `C` opens the SYSTEMS panel (a row's digit moves a pip; panel data in `CAMP_PANELS`: `{ key: 'C', code: 'KeyC', side: 'left', slot: 1 }`, a panel with a `code` opens on that letter in `campPanelKey`); the toast and the voice say "Power to shields." / "Power balanced." / "Emergency shields!"; the computer says "Shields are low, pilot." under 30 percent of the cap (again after 60) and "Engines are low on power, pilot." when the engines are set to 1 pip or 0; the radio calls both (`shields`, `engines`). The Fast burn and the map's flight time and the autopilot's top speed read the engines.
- **Does it matter?** (`node tools/campaign-bot.mjs --level 1,3 --seeds 6 --bots plain --power w,s,e`; the plain bot does not manage power, it only plays the setting): the default `2,2,2` is **byte for byte the result of before**. On level 3 (the towers): `4,1,1` wins in 124 s (139 s at the default) and takes 54 damage (113); `3,3,0` 120 s and 82; `1,1,4` 129 s and 138; `1,4,1` 139 s and 177; `0,3,3` 147 s and 189. Level 1 is the same whatever the setting (an easy level: the bot never needs it). Weapons up is the strongest single lever against towers and the mothership; shields and engines are for a player who reads the fight (and the next cards: outposts, pursuit).
- **Not in this card:** power for the wing, a per-system heat or damage, a power that costs ore, new keys beyond C.
- **Pinned by** `tests/campsystems.mjs` (plain Node): the table, the default equal to the numbers of before (a battle replayed, bit for bit), each effect, the budget rule, the emergency and its cooldown, the save.
## Scout-escorts (card `space-game-campaign-scouts`; Troy: "could the escorts be scouts/escorts? in missions they can do recon work, escort, get caches")
*Design lines, written before the code.* An escort can be sent out as a **scout** with the **Scout order**: one escort (the nearest one that is out and free) flies to a place at its own speed, stays there a few seconds **reading**, reports, and comes back; "Scout one is going to Mars." ... "Scout one, nothing at Mars." Everything is one table, `CAMP.scout` in `src/campaign.js`; a scout is an escort whose `order` is `{ type: 'scout' }` and whose `u.scout` holds its name, its place, its phase (out, station, home) and what it carries.
- **How you give it:** the third button on the Tab map beside Fly and Fast (click a place, a recon point or a cache, then **Scout**, the key V), the strip and the voice ("scout Mars", "send a scout to the relic"), and `campOrder(C, 'scout', 'mars')` (a closed-list act, `fleet_scout`). One order sends one scout (so two escorts are two scouts, at two places); a scout needs an escort that is out of the bay and not already scouting ("No escort is free to scout."). A fleet panel row waits for 6-GEN's stances (not here).
- **Sensors:** a scout that is out has a **sensor circle** (`sense` 2,600 px) round it: the map draws it, the radar marks the scout, and a cloaked anomaly inside `read` (1,400 px) is **read** from afar ("Anomaly: likely weapons cache", the table is 6-GEN's anomalies card; the hook is `C.anomalies` with `{ x, y, cloaked, likely }`): only a visit by the carrier or the wing uncovers it.
- **Recon:** a scout within a recon point's radius plus `scanReach` (260 px) fills the point **2.5 times as fast** as the wing does (a point of 8 s takes 3.2 s): the mission counts it, the carrier need not fly to each point.
- **Fetch (decided: it counts when the scout is back aboard):** a scout out touches a cache, an ore chunk or a wreck from `pickup` (90 px), up to `carry` (60 ore; a cache is a load of its own) and takes it home; when it lands in the bay the ore is in the store and the cache is gathered (the mission counts it). A scout lost on the way **drops** what it carries (the ore lies where it died, the cache is back where it was). A scout that carries a full load goes home by itself.
- **Fragile and lost:** a scout out takes damage **2.5 times** over ("fragile": an escort's 90 hit points feel like 36); it is a ship lost like any other (`C.lostShips`; the "clean" bonus counts it). It does not fight and does not guard the carrier (a Guard or Attack order does not take it from its task; Recall brings it home). Rebuilding it is the outposts card's.
- **Left behind on Fast:** a scout farther than `leash` (5,200 px) from the carrier when the carrier is on a Fast burn is **left behind: lost** ("A scout was left behind."). Bring it home first (Recall) or stay inside the leash. *Since the Fast rule of card `space-game-campaign-fast-board` (a burn needs the wing aboard: a first press with a ship out is refused, a second recalls the wing and burns when the last one is aboard) a normal Fast never leaves a scout behind: the scouts are recalled with the wing and land with their load. The leash stays as the safety net for a burn that is on anyway (`campScoutsStep`).*
- **Missions say which tasks they allow (data, not code):** a mission row may have `scouts: ['recon', 'escort', 'fetch']` (all three when it says nothing); the Scout order needs `recon` or `fetch`; the scan speed-up needs `recon`; picking up needs `fetch`; `escort` is the escorts' convoy order as before. No row is changed except to show the data.
- **Not changed:** with no Scout order given, **nothing** is different (the pinned battles of `tests/campsystems.mjs`-style golden runs are the same: `tests/campscouts.mjs` pins it).
- **Shown:** a count of scouts out on the HUD ("SCOUTS 1"), chatter lines for scouts, a few words in the ship's voice.
- **Built (what it is in the code):** `CAMP.scout` and the `campScout*` functions in `src/campaign.js` (the order `campOrder(C, 'scout', 'vesta' | a map row)`, `campScoutAI`, `campScoutsStep`, `campScoutDock`, `campScoutDrop`, `campFree`, `campSensors`); mission rows `scouts: [...]` (survey: recon, fetch; haulers: escort, fetch; mothership: recon, fetch; resources: recon, fetch; approach: recon; the others say nothing: all three); the third map button and the V key (`campMapScout` in `src/campmapdraw.js`, with the sensor circles); `fleet_scout` in the computer's closed list ("scout Mars", "send a scout to the relic"); a scout's dot is pale cyan on the radar and the map (`campDotColor`); the radio calls `scouts` (a report) and `scoutlost` (left behind); the words are `content/text/campaign-scouts.md`. A scout is numbered by the order it was sent in (Scout 1, Scout 2: `C.scoutN`).
- **Anomalies (tied in to the anomalies card's `C.anoms`, `src/campanomaly.js`):** a scout within `CAMP.scout.read` (1,400 px) of a **hidden** (cloaked) anomaly **notices** it: `a.sensed` becomes true (the mark appears on the radar and the map, as when the carrier comes within 2,400 px), `a.read` is set, the map and the nav list name it "Anomaly: likely {a.hides}" (a weapons store, a tech lab), the computer says "Anomaly: likely {likely}." once, the radio calls `anomaly`, and the scout's report says what it read. It never decloaks it: only the carrier's visit (`CAMP.anomaly.visit` for `hold` seconds) does, as before.
- **Waiting for others:** a fleet-panel row for Scout (6-GEN's stances page), rebuilding a lost scout at an outpost (an outposts row for it).

## How it is built

- `src/campaign.js` is the whole game in plain numbers, no Phaser: the level table, the ships, the waves, the five orders, the aliens' flying (the game's own `pirateSteer` and `PIRATES` from `src/enemies.js`), win and lose, the tech pick and its save. `tests/campaign.mjs` runs whole battles of it in Node.
- `src/campaignscene.js` is a thin scene: it reads the keys, steps the game, and draws what the numbers say. It has the only Phaser in the mode and is not run by a test.
- The command strip, the ship computer and the voice reach it through the closed list (`SC_ACT`): `fleet_launch`, `fleet_attack`, `fleet_escort`, `fleet_guard`, `fleet_recall`. The words are read by the game itself, with no chat key needed.
- Story text is in `content/text/ui.md` (keys `camp.*`), so it can be translated or changed without code.

## Assumed, and open

- The playing field is a 1 to 12 scale of the real Sol distances (Earth to Mars is about 4,100 px), not the true scale: true scale would make a level an hour.
- The campaign has no money: the convoys that arrive only appear in the story line after the level.
- Alpha Centauri and Epsilon Eridani are my picks; the alien home world has no real name.
- The turrets' reach is 500 px, about three and a half carrier lengths (the carrier is drawn 98 px long). With the single gun gone, the carrier's only far reach is the missile. The numbers (500 px, 4 a shot, every 0.7 s, eight turrets: 46 a second) were fitted to the sensible players of `tests/campaign.mjs` twice: first with shots aimed where the alien is (80 a second), then, when `space-game-campaign-turret-lead` made the shots hit moving aliens, lowered, because a shot that lands is worth more than one that misses. A carrier that stays at the Moon and holds Space still wins level 1 in 2.5 to 5 minutes; with the old, harder numbers (Earth's shield 300, no mending) it wins at most half the seeds. One table, `CAMP.carrier`.
- Missiles are for big ships on purpose: with only raiders and drones near, F answers "No big ship in missile range" and spends nothing.
- Left out of this cut: a map between levels, saving a level in the middle, difficulty, music of its own, and more than three techs a level.
