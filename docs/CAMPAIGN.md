# Campaign: Earth's fleet against the aliens (the first cut)

Card `space-game-campaign-mvp`. A new mode beside Long Haul: the title screen offers both as two panels, a click each: **Long Haul** (Enter; since the card `space-game-title-any-key` not any other key: a mode starts only on its own key, the arrows and Enter, or a click on its panel) and the **Campaign** (**K**); `?campaign=N` in the address still opens a level at once. The pause menu of either game goes back to the title: Long Haul's has a **Title screen** row (it saves first, as the pause menu always does; New game stays last) and the Campaign's pause card has **T**; from the title either game starts again (card `space-game-title-modes`, words in `content/text/title.md`). Long Haul, its trading and its save are not touched. The aim is that Troy plays all three levels fast, a few minutes each, and feels the story.

**Status (20 Nov 2026).** Level 1 is now the asteroid belt (card `space-game-campaign-belt`, Troy's new story): the chain is the belt, the Earth refit, Alpha Centauri, the home world, an ending. The Earth defence level (Earth's shield, the Mars and Eros convoys) is out of the chain; its rules still work and it is kept as data under `CAMP.levels.home` for the tests and for a later campaign. The rules are tested in Node (`tests/campaign.mjs`; a plain bot, `tools/campaign-bot.mjs`, wins level 1 in about four to four and a half minutes, a passive player loses). The look, the radar and the feel are seen only by whoever boots it in Chrome. Every number is a line of the `CAMP` table at the top of `src/campaign.js`, and a level is a row of it.

**The story.** The cruiser is a mining ship working the asteroid belt at Ceres, out of the Occator Works. Its fighters are its mining drones, its eight turrets are mining lasers. A quiet minute of mining, then an alien fleet ambushes the belt out of the dark. The mining tools are turned to weapons (the story says so; the keys do not change). Three waves and a mothership later the belt is clear; the cruiser flies home, Earth refits it with one of three alien techs (and the ore it mined buys extra ships), and it warps to Alpha Centauri, then to the alien home world. All the story lines are curt (one short sentence, about 12 words at most, fact first: Troy's rule), shown and spoken.

## Missions (card `space-game-campaign-missions`; Troy: "steal from X-Wing: recon, escort, attack, defend")

Each level is a run of three short missions, each with a **briefing** (one screen: its title, the objectives in plain words, the bonus objectives), the **flight**, and a **debrief** (the score, the bonus objectives met, time, hull, ships lost, and the rank when the ranks card is there). A mission lost is flown again (the level is not lost); a mission can be restarted from the pause card (R); after the last mission of a level come the level's story and, in the belt, the Earth refit. The mission table is data, apart from the code that runs it, so a campaign can be made again and again.

**The table.** `CAMP.levels[n].missions` is a list of rows. A row is laid over its level's row (`campMissionRow(level, i)`, which a battle reads as `C.L`), so it may set anything the level sets (`waves`, `convoys`, `rocks`, `guards`, `towers`, `caches`, `relic`, `start`, `startAt`, `defend` ...) and these fields of its own:

| field | what it is |
|---|---|
| `id`, `kind` | its name, and its kind: `recon`, `escort`, `attack`, `defend`, `waves` (the kind is for the hub and the words; what runs is the objectives) |
| `objectives` | what the mission asks; ALL must be met: `scan` (points `{ at, r, secs }`: any ship of yours within r of each for secs), `deliver` (`n` haulers arrive and none is still on its way), `kill` (`what`: `mother`, `towers`, `guards`, `wave:<i>`), `hold` (`secs`: the defended place stands that long), `relic`, `caches` (`n`), `clear`, `ore` (`n`: the store held that much at some time), `cashin` (`n` ore sold at a market outpost) |
| `bonus` | bonus objectives, each worth `CAMP.score.bonus`: `time` (`secs`), `lost` (`n` ships at most), `hull` (`pct` at least), `ore` (`n`), `sold` (`n` ore sold at a market), `delivered` (every hauler) |
| `par` | the seconds it should take (else `CAMP.score.par`): each second under it is worth `CAMP.score.speed` |
| `waves[].on` | a wave that waits for an event instead of a time: `{ scanned: k }`, `{ delivered: k }`, `{ t: s }` (the ambush that hits mid-survey), `{ lane: f }` (a convoy has flown that share of its road; `ahead`: the wave comes from that many px in front of the convoy) |
| `convoys[]` | haulers `{ id, name, say, at, from, to, speed? }`: `from` and `to` are places of the level, `name` its words ("Hauler 1") |
| `defend` | `{ place, shield, name }`: a place with a shield the aliens go for (an Earth-like thing, `C.earth`); lost, the reason is `base` |

A level row with no `missions` is one mission of its own (kind `waves`, no objectives: won by the old rule), which is how the Earth level `home` and the tests' old rows still work. The words: `camp.m.<level>.<mission>.title`, `.b1`, `.b2` (two curt lines), the objective lines `camp.o.*`, the bonus lines `camp.b.*`, the debrief `camp.ui.*`: all in `content/text/campaign-missions.md`.

**The state** (`campProgressLoad` / `campProgressSave`, one JSON under `longhaul.campaign`): `level` and `mission` (the next to fly), `techs`, `ore` and `bonus` (carried into the next mission or level), and `results`: `{ '<level>.<mission>': { score, bonuses: [ids], t, hull, lost, tries } }`, the best score of each mission flown. The API the hub, the ranks and the chatter build on: `campMissions(level)`, `campMissionRow(level, i)`, `campResultKey(level, i)`, `campMissionDone(P, level, i, score)` (keeps the result, moves on, answers `{ levelDone }`), `campMissionTried`, and in a battle `campObjectives(C)` (each objective's state), `campBonuses(C)`, `campScore(C)` (`{ score, base, bonuses, bonus, hull, speed, lost, t, hullLeft, shipsLost }`, also `C.score` when won). The ranks card hangs a rank on the score with a global `campRankLine(P, score)`, which the debrief calls when it exists. A start can ask for a mission: `campNew(level, techs, { mission })`, the scene `go(this, 'Campaign', { level, mission })`; only a level's first mission warps (src/campwarp.js).

**Mission places** (card `space-game-campaign-mission-places`; Troy: "have reasons for needing to use most of the solar system"). A mission row has a `place` (a body of its level's places: the mission happens there), a `dock` (the body whose outpost the debrief returns you to; the place's `outpost` name, from `CAMP.moons` or the level's `outposts`), and its points (`rocks`, `caches`, `guards`, scan points, `waves[].from`/`mother`) are **offsets from the place**, made absolute in `campMissionRow`. Towers use `place` of their own (`towers[].place`), convoys `from`/`to` bodies.

- **The bodies.** The Sol pack gives level 1 the planets (far, drawn when you come near; `src/campsol.js`). `CAMP.moons` adds the moons a level lists in `moons: [ids]`: Luna, Phobos, Deimos, Io, Europa, Ganymede, Callisto, Titan, Enceladus (level 1) and Ran, Kolga, Tyr (level 3); each is `{ id, of (its planet), name, at (px from it), kind, seed, r, outpost }`. A moon is added once its planet is there, and only when the level lists it.
- **The spread.** Level 1: Ceres (the cruiser starts there: no trip), Mars and its moons (the haulers), Europa of Jupiter (the mothership). Level 2: Alpha Centauri B's world, Proxima d, Proxima b; dock at Proxima w. Level 3: Kolga, Ran, the alien world; dock at Tyr. Every level uses at least four bodies (places, docks and where the haulers go; `tests/campaign.mjs` section Q).
- **The trip.** The cruiser starts every mission at its level's start; when that is further than `CAMP.mission.arrive` (3,000 px) from the mission's place, the mission has a trip. The objective line says "Fly to Mars: 2,840 km." (a km is 10 px); the map marks the place as the objective (a gold ring; the view takes it in) and the nav goal is the place, so click-to-fly or Fast takes you there on the map's own rule (`campNavBurn`: twice the speed, 3.2 times beyond 8,000 px, the wing out towed along). `campTravel(level, i)` is the one place that says the distance and the seconds, for the briefing: `PLACE  Mars     2840 km     about 46 s by Fast`.
- **The clock.** Nothing in the mission begins before the cruiser is within 3,000 px of the place: no wave, no hauler, no par time, no hold. From then on `campMissionClock(C)` counts (waves, convoys, the swift bonus, the score's time all read it). The arrival says "Arrived at Mars." and lets the autopilot go. Launch the wing after arriving; a Fast trip leaves it behind.
- **The debrief** says "Docked at Arsia High, Phobos." (`camp.ui.outpost`).
- **No pack.** If a place is not there (the Sol pack is removed) the mission happens where the cruiser starts and its haulers go between the first two places: nothing throws.
- **For the next cards.** The hub, outposts and anomalies read `place`, `dock` and `CAMP.moons`; the anomalies card spreads them over the same bodies.

**The score.** `CAMP.score`: 1,000 for the objectives, 250 for each bonus objective, up to 300 for the hull left, 5 a second under the par, minus 50 for each ship lost.

**The nine missions.** The belt: 1 *Belt survey* (recon: scan three rock fields; the ambush hits when the second is scanned and must be beaten), 2 *Ore haulers* (escort: three haulers fly from Deimos to Phobos, at Mars; two must arrive, the raiders go for them), 3 *The mothership* (attack: three waves, the last with the mothership, at Europa). Alpha Centauri: 1 *Resources* (recon: two of three caches, and the attackers who followed), 2 *The guards* (attack: seven guards round the relic), 3 *The relic* (defend: hold its ring 14 s with no alien near it). The home world: 1 *Approach* (recon: three points on the way in; the defenders come on the first), 2 *Outer ring* (attack: four towers), 3 *Inner ring* (attack: eight towers). A plain bot (`node tools/campaign-bot.mjs`, which flies the missions one by one, `--mission N` for one) wins each in 45 to 75 seconds of fight (the trip to a far place is on top: 46 s to Mars, 37 s to Europa by Fast); a person takes longer; a player who does nothing never wins one (the survey and the approach are never finished, the others are lost).

**What the goal line writes (cards `space-game-12-sell-goal-count` and `space-game-12-goal-sold-aboard`).** `campObjectives(C)` gives each objective `got` (the rule's: done is `got >= need`; the goal line and the panel's value write it) and `aboard` (the ore in the store, for the sale only: `cashin`, 1.2, sell 160 ore at the Occator Works; 0 for every other goal). The sale's `got` is the ore SOLD (`C.sold`, `campSell`): a dock sells the whole store at once and the sale ends the mission, so the number reads 0 / 160 while ore is carried and 160 / 160 only when it is sold (Troy, 26 Nov 2026: never full before the sale). The page HUD's goal meter shows the ore sold as the solid fill and the ore aboard as a faint fill after it, capped at the target (`campPageHudGoal`'s `ghost`, the `ph-ghost` element behind the solid fill), so the meter moves while mining. Nothing is won on the ore carried. `tests/campgoalcount.mjs` runs a scripted mission for each kind of goal and reads the line and the panel. The goal panel's second line (`campPageHudHint`, `src/camppagehud.js`) is, while the first goal not done is a sale and the store holds ore, `camp.hud.aboard` ("{n} ore aboard · dock to sell", the dock's key as a chip) wherever the cruiser is (card `space-game-sell-goal-aboard-line`); otherwise it is as it was: the retreat line first, else the dock line (`campDockHint`, only within `CAMP.outpost.range` of an outpost and when docking is worth it).

## Ranks (card `space-game-campaign-ranks`)

You are promoted as you fly. Six ranks, plain names: **Cadet, Ensign, Lieutenant, Commander, Captain, Commodore**. The points are the **best scores of the missions flown, added up** (a mission score already counts its objectives, its bonus objectives, the hull left and the time; a mission flown again for a better score can only raise the total). Nothing is saved for the rank: it is read from the saved results.

| Rank | From points | Extra wing strength (it was fighters + escorts before card `space-game-cut-escorts`) | About |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cadet | 0 | 0 | the start |
| Ensign | 1,000 | 1 | one good mission |
| Lieutenant | 3,500 | 3 (2 + 1) | about three missions |
| Commander | 6,500 | 4 (3 + 1) | about five |
| Captain | 10,000 | 6 (4 + 2) | about seven |
| Commodore | 14,000 | 8 (5 + 3) | the nine missions flown well |

The effect is on the wing you command: the extras are added to the **bay** at the start of every mission (on top of the bay of eight fighters, the second-bay tech and the extras of the Earth refit) and to the **wing limit** of the workshop (14 fighters and 6 escorts, plus the extras), so the orders (launch, attack, escort, guard, recall) matter more as you rise. The debrief says the promotion when a mission's score takes you up (`PROMOTED: Lieutenant. The bay holds 8 fighters and 3 escorts.` and a line of the rank's own), and the rank and what the next needs otherwise; the hub (the ready room) shows the rank through `campRankStatus(P)`: `{ name, n, of, points, next, nextName, wing: { fighters, escorts } }`.

The ranks are data: `CAMP.ranks` in `src/campaign.js` (the points and the extras of each), the code is `src/campranks.js`, the words are `content/text/campaign-ranks.md`. A battle is made with `campNew(level, techs, { rank })` (the scene passes the rank of the saved results; no rank is a cadet, so every old battle of a test is as it was). **Balance**: `node tools/campaign-bot.mjs --rank 0|1..5|auto` flies the missions at a rank (auto: 1,400 points a mission flown before it). The missions were already won by every bot at the lowest rank, so the ranks do not change who wins; at the top rank the wing takes about half the damage on the hard missions (the home world's second and third: 60 to 29 and 30 to 18 on average) and the missions end 5 to 10 percent sooner. A battle with no action still loses at every rank (`tests/campranks.mjs`).

**Left for later (the idea bench, `space-game-idea-bench`): a second carrier at a high rank.** Not built; the table has no room for one yet on purpose.

## The ready room: the hub between missions (card `space-game-campaign-hub`; Troy: "I do like how the pilots start out in the space station")

The Campaign starts every mission from a station, like X-Wing's pilots: the **ready room** (scene `CampHub`, `src/camphubscene.js`; the model is `src/camphub.js`, pure, `tests/camphub.mjs`). The title's Campaign choice (K) opens it, and so does every debrief (Enter) and the end of every level (after the story and the refit or pick). It is Long Haul's own station hall (`src/interior.js`, the same baked floor, lights, hologram desks, repair arm and hangar), in the colour of the level's station: the belt outpost (Occator Works), the warp station at Alpha Centauri (Proxima Gate), the last outpost before the home world (Aegir Watch). Nobody walks: the pilot stands at the desk of the row that is chosen, and the cruiser stands on the pad.

Four rows, kept in view at the left of a glass panel; at the right **a sentence or two** says what the chosen row is (`camp.room.desc.<id>`, `content/text/readyroom.md`); an amber line under the rows says what Enter does. **Enter (or Right) opens the row as an OVERLAY** that takes the top of the screen (card `space-game-ready-room-simple`; Troy, 21 Nov 2026: the old right-hand pages listed options he could not read, and the Briefing row said what Enter on a mission already does). **Up and Down** move in the overlay's list (the same on every one), **Enter** does the thing on the choice, **Left or Esc** closes it.

| key | row | what Enter opens |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | MISSIONS | every level and its missions at the left (flown ones with their score and bonus objectives, the next one marked NEXT) and the briefing of the one under the cursor beside them; **Enter** (or **R**) opens the big three-screen briefing, a mission done is a replay and so is one SKIPPED (`[-]`, see below), **X** goes back to the next mission; the last row, **Start the campaign over**, asks on Enter and does it on the second Enter |
| 2 | REFIT | the ore and the wing, then each choice (a fighter, an escort) with **what it changes in numbers** ("Fighters in your wing: 6 to 7"), **what it costs** and **what you have**, marked when you can buy it; **Enter** buys the choice under the cursor (or **F**, **E**, **U** at once), **Backspace** gives the last one back; the tech already fitted is listed with its numbers (`campHubTechChange`: "Carrier hull: 600 to 900", from `CAMP.tech`); every purchase is saved at once |
| 3 | RANK | the rank and the score, from the ranks card's `campRankStatus(P)` (`src/campranks.js`) when it is there, the points to the next rank, and every rank with the points it is held from and the ships it adds to the wing |
| 4 | LAUNCH | (no overlay) **Enter** here launches; its sentence names the mission, or the replay. **L** anywhere opens the briefing of that mission first (card `space-game-ready-l-briefing-first`, Troy, 25 Nov 2026), and **L** again, in the briefing, launches |

The pick and the refit after a level list each tech the same way: its name and what it changes with the numbers. The keys (a digit picks and closes an overlay, the rows stay in view): **W S** or **Up Down** move the row, **Enter** or **Right** opens it, **L** opens the briefing of the mission Launch flies, from anywhere (a second **L**, in the briefing, launches it; in a dock **L** undocks at once), **Esc** closes an open overlay and with none open (or **T**) leaves for the title (nothing is lost: the state is saved at every debrief and every purchase). A click on a row picks it; a second click opens it, or launches on LAUNCH. Launching rolls the hangar door up and starts the Campaign scene on the mission with `via: 'hub'`: the briefing was read in the room, so the scene shows only the level's story (at a level's first mission, after the warp cinematic where there is one) and flies. A lost mission is still flown again from its card (`via: 'retry'`), not through the room. A dock's room (`ctx.dock`) has no overlays: its rows keep their pages and the old keys.

**The room is data, so a dock can open it** (card `space-game-campaign-outposts`: building ships and repairing the hull happen docked at a friendly outpost, and docking opens this room). A row is its id and: its words (`camp.hub.row.<id>`, `camp.hub.foot.<id>`, in `content/text/camphub.md` and `UI_TEXT`), a note (`CAMP_HUB.note[id](P)`), what Enter does on it (`CAMP_HUB.act[id]`: an action name, or nothing to go on to the next row), its own keys (`CAMP_HUB.rowKey[id](H, P, code, res)`), and in the scene its page (`CAMP_HUBS.page[id](scene)`, drawn with `scene.line(i, col, text, color)`), or, in the hall, its sentence (`CAMP_HUBS.desc[id]`) and its overlay (`CAMP_HUB.over[id]` for the list, the cursor, what Enter does and the overlay's own keys; `CAMP_HUBS.overlay[id](scene)` draws it with `scene.ovLine`) and where the pilot stands (`CAMP_HUBS.stops[id]`, `.face[id]`); an action's doing is `CAMP_HUBS.run[action](scene)`. The scene is started with `{ rows: [ids], at: id, station: { name, accent, art }, onLaunch(scene), onLeave(scene) }`, all optional: `rows` is the list of rows of this visit (a dock might open `['build', 'repair', 'refit', 'board']`), `onLaunch` replaces "start the next mission" (a dock's way back to the flight it came from), `onLeave` replaces "the title". The refit's F, E and Backspace work in any room that has the `refit` row. `tests/camphubscene.mjs` (section E2) opens a room with a registered row as the example.

**Replaying a mission** (card `space-game-campaign-replay`; Troy: "it would be nice to be able to replay the missions completed"). In the missions overlay, every mission with a result shows its best score, the bonus objectives found of all and (with the ranks) the rank its best run gave. **Up and Down** move a `>` over the missions done, **Enter** or **R** picks the one under it (`[R]`) and opens its briefing (headed REPLAY), **X** goes back to the next mission. With a replay picked the Launch row says `replay 2`, and **Launch** flies it from its standard start, **with your current wing, rank, techs, extras and ore**. The rules: the **story and progress do not move** (the level and the mission to fly next stay, no story card, no warp, no refit, no tech pick at the end: the debrief goes back to the ready room); the result **keeps the best score** and the **bonus objectives found are every one any run met**; the ranks' points are the sum of the best scores, so **a replay raises them only by what it beat the old best by** (flying the same score again farms nothing); the ore comes with the cruiser as ever; one-time rewards are not given twice (the level-end pick and refit are never part of a replay, and a looted anomaly cache is already off the map); **losing a replay costs nothing** (the result is untouched) and R at the pause or Enter at the lose card flies it again as a replay. The debrief is titled REPLAY and says `this run 1,430, your best was 640. A new best: 1,430 points.`

**Click a mission** (card `space-game-click-mission`; Troy, 24 Nov 2026). With the missions overlay open, a click on a mission (the ones done, skipped and the next, the cursor's own rule) moves the cursor there as an arrow does; a second click on the chosen one is `Enter` (pick it, open its briefing). A hand and a faint bar show the missions that can be picked; Start over stays a key. The rules are `campHubBoardHits` and `campHubClickMission` in `src/camphub.js`, the click zones are the scene's (`src/camphubscene.js`).

**Skipped missions, an old save and starting over** (card `space-game-replay-reselect-bug`; Troy's save sat on level 3 with every earlier mission marked `[-]`: the missions had changed under an older save, so they had no result, and the cursor stood only on the ones done and the next). A mission **before the next one with no result is SKIPPED**: the cursor reaches it like a done one, **Enter** or **R** picks it and opens its briefing (headed as a mission, with "Not flown yet" under it), **Launch** flies it as a replay (`replay` in the launch data: the story, the warp and the progress do not move; `campHubTarget` says `skipped: true`), and **its first win is kept as its result** (the row turns `[x]` with its score, the debrief is a plain debrief, since there is no best to compare with). Missions not reached yet (`[ ]`) stay out of reach. A save like that is told **once**, under the sentence of the ready room, in amber: *This save is from an older version. Missions that changed show as skipped [-]: pick one in Missions and fly it, and a win counts as its score.* (the flag is a key of its own, `longhaul.campaign.oldsave`, beside the save and not in it). The last row of the Missions list is **Start the campaign over**: **Enter** asks (the hint line turns amber: *Enter again: yes. Any other key: no.*), the second **Enter** begins again at mission 1.1 (the level, the mission, the scores, techs, ore, extra ships and the found caches go; the ending seen stays), and the scene saves at once. The overlay's key line says how to fly one again (`Enter open its briefing, then Launch flies it`) and a legend under the list says what `[x] [-] [>] [ ]` mean. (or `Your best stands at 640 points.`). A mission of a level you have not finished can be replayed once it is done. The data is the mission's result in the save (`longhaul.campaign` results: `score, bonuses, t, hull, lost, tries` and now `replays` and `rank`; the top-level keys are unchanged). H in the room opens the page of the room's keys (the READY ROOM group of the Controls table).

**The big briefing** (card `space-game-campaign-briefing`; Troy: "the briefing could come up automatically after selecting a mission, and it can take up more space: have the solar system map, the goals, a walk-through"; then, on his play, card `space-game-briefing-walkthrough`: "zoom in and out, label it Mission 1, three screens to walk through with pictures"). **Enter** or **R** on the board picks the mission under the cursor and opens its briefing **at once** (a mission done is the replay; the next mission is the road), as a screen over most of the window, headed **MISSION 1** and its title (not "1 of 3": which of how many is the board's business), with the names of its three screens under the heading, the one you are on lit. **Enter** or **Right** goes on, **Left** goes back, **Enter** on the last screen launches, **L** launches from any (it is the table's `ready` key, `CAMP_KEYS.ready`, `src/data_controls.js`: the first **L** in the room opens this briefing, the second launches), **Esc** is back to the board. Every other key is the screen's: nothing under it moves. It does not open in an outpost's dock room (nothing there launches a mission). The ship reads the **first walkthrough step once** when the briefing opens, in its voice and by the voice's own settings (muted, off or no voice is silent; no new setting): that line is the voice pack's source `campaign.walk`.
1. **WHERE AND WHY.** At the left the system map (the Tab map's own pieces: the grid, the rings round the star, the belts as rocks, the places and the outposts, `src/campmapdraw.js`' helpers) with the mission's place (the row's `place`, the one the HUD's "Fly to <place>" names) marked in amber, **YOU** where the carrier starts, the dotted route between them and the objective points: scan points as rings (numbered), the haulers' lane as an arrow, the resource pods, the guards, the defence towers, the relic's ring, where the mothership comes in. At the right the place, the **route** said once (one distance: `campTravel` in `src/campaign.js`, with `campKmText`, to the centre of the place, the same number the HUD says; the flight on regular speed and on Fast; a mission that starts at its place has none) and the **story line** (the level's own lines before its first mission, then the mission's two). **=**, **-** and **Backspace** zoom the map as they zoom the Tab map (the numpad's + and - too, a held key repeats, a press is the same step, 0.5 to 8 times, Backspace resets) and so does the **mouse wheel** over the briefing; the zoom is about the mission's place, which stays where it is on the glass (`campBriefView`, which is `campMapView` of the Tab map), and its percentage reads in the corner of the map as the Tab map's does. **M** goes round three views: the **route** (it opens on this: the start, the place and the points), the **mission area** (the place and its points, close) and the **whole system**, skipping a view that looks the same as the one it leaves, and puts the zoom back to 100% (M does not also mute the sound while the briefing is up: `src/meta.js`).
2. **WHAT TO DO.** The **goals as steps**, numbered, each with a small **picture** made of the game's own sprites (`campBriefPicture`: a fighter in a scan ring, raiders and a drone in a reticle, the haulers on their way to a place, pods, the Reaver for the mothership, the gunship tinted for a tower, the carrier in the relic's ring), then the **bonus objectives** with their points and a smaller picture each (a clock, the wing in a green ring, the cruiser mining a rock, the cruiser and its hull bar, the haulers, the cloaked pod): the words are the HUD's own (`campHubBrief`: the goals and bonus lines of the Briefing row), so the picture is of what the line says.
3. **HOW.** The **walkthrough** of three to six curt steps, the **keys this mission needs** as key caps with what they do (`campBriefKeys`: the basics for every mission, in the order they are used: the arrow keys, Space, the order key W (attack the picked enemy, guard the picked friendly), Enter (the missile), Tab; then what its goals and bonus ask for: S (the cruiser list: Mine ore) for an ore goal or bonus, S (Dock at the outpost) for a cash-in, S (Emergency shields) for a hull bonus; the zoom last; at most eight. An order of the cruiser list shows S and names the row), the **enemies** to expect (what the waves, guards and towers add up to) and your **readiness** (the bay, the rank, the missiles, the ore, the power setting saved in this browser and the shield with its cap).
- **The walkthrough is data.** The lines are `camp.bf.<level>.<mission>.w<n>` in `content/text/campbrief.md` (no more than twelve words each, no spoilers: nothing of the cloaked caches or the reveal), fixed lines the voice pack records; a mission without them shows one plain line and says nothing. `tests/campbrief.mjs` checks that the numbers in each line (scan points and seconds, haulers and the gap, pods, guards, the hold, towers, when the waves come) are the ones in the mission's data, so a change to the data without the words fails there.
- **Built as two halves.** `src/campbrief.js` is the pure model (layers, bounds, route, the zoom's view, the screens' keys, the goals' pictures, the keys a mission needs, the story, the walkthrough, enemies, readiness; plain Node), `src/campbriefdraw.js` draws it and takes the keys, `src/camphub.js` / `src/camphubscene.js` open it (the board's cursor now stands on the missions done **and the next one**, starting on the next). `tests/campbrief.mjs` runs all of it in Node on a recording stand-in (`tests/briefstand.mjs`); `tests/campbrief-boot.mjs` is the real game with one picture.
- **Readiness.** The wing is always in the bay and the shield full in the ready room, so those are lines, never warnings; the power setting (card `space-game-campaign-systems`) is a line too.
- **The walking map, for a mission that has a walk** (card `space-game-briefing-map-walk`; Troy, 22 Nov 2026: "the mission map should be used for each page of the briefing, zoomed as needed, with my own craft walking through the plan"; mission 1.1 first). A mission with a walk (a row in `src/data_briefwalk.js`, or a `walk` of its own mission row) is briefed on **the map on all three pages**, the same three page names under the same heading. The map fills the window under the heading; the **cruiser** (white), its **wing** (cyan) and the **convoy** (amber) walk the road between the two places the convoy leaves and goes to, stopping at each **stop**; the camera eases to where the stop happens (the whole road on page 1; closer in on pages 2 and 3); under the map are the dots of the stops (the one you are at is gold), **one line of words**, and a small strip: the story on page 1, the goals and the bonus on page 2, the keys this mission needs and your readiness on page 3. A page **plays its stops by itself** and waits at its last one; **Enter** or **Right** goes on a stop (to the next page at the last stop of a page), **Left** goes back, **Enter** at the last stop of the last page or **L** launches, **Esc** closes, **+ - Backspace** and the wheel zoom the map by hand on every page (the walk holds while it is zoomed). Marks are **hints**, never facts: a dashed ring with "PIRATES?" where raiders are likely, a ring with "CALL?" where a distress call may come from, brackets with "ARRIVE"; a stop's own marks are bright, the earlier ones dim. 1.1's stops: leave Luna with the patrol, the patrol peels off (12 percent of the road, the row's own `escort.until`), a distress call may come (the miner the row has off the road), pirates are likely to ambush the road, the pirate leader waits near Ceres (its `on` share and `ahead`), arrive. A stop's place is **data**, read from the mission's own rows: `{ road: share }` (the shares `on: { lane }` use), `{ place }`, `{ spot: <traffic id> }`, `{ rival: <rival id> }`, so a retune of those rows moves the walk with it. The other eight missions keep the three screens; adding one is a row of data and its lines (`camp.bf.<level>.<mission>.s<n>`, in `content/text/campwalk.md`), nothing else. `src/campbriefwalk.js` is the pure half (plan, time, keys; `tests/campbriefwalk.mjs`, plain Node), `src/campbriefwalkdraw.js` draws it, and `tests/campbriefwalk-boot.mjs` is the real game with a picture of each stop. On a walking map **M does nothing** (it is the three-screen map's key).

## Outposts: dock to sell ore and refit, with half-price repairs (card `space-game-campaign-outposts`; Troy: a reason to retreat and regroup across the solar system; changed by card `space-game-repair-with-ore`, Troy, 22 Nov 2026: "we do not build ships, we repair them in space with ore")

**The rule (since card `space-game-repair-with-ore`).** **Nothing builds ships any more**: the wing you launch with is the wing you have for the mission, and the next mission starts with the whole wing again. **Ore mends instead, in open space** (`src/campmend.js`, the numbers `CAMP.mend` and `CAMP.repair.ore`): the dock and repair list (`U`) is **Dock at the outpost** (only where there is an outpost), **Repair the hull** (the ore is paid when the order is given, the hull mends over a few seconds: the price is the share of the hull that is missing of **60 ore for a whole hull**, at least 5, and a whole hull takes **8 s**; at an outpost it is instant and **half price**) and **Repair a ship we protect** (the transport, a hauler or a miner within **700 px** of the cruiser and hurt: **0.1 ore a hit point**, a whole 200-point freighter in **6 s**; one hurt ship in range is taken at once, several open a list, which shows each one's price). A **hurt fighter** that lands is still mended (Repair, below) but each mend costs **5 ore**, and with none it takes **twice as long** and is never refused. **In flight everything is paid in ORE only**; credits from a sale are spent at a station (docked, the dock's repair takes them first). "build a fighter" typed or spoken is still understood: the ship answers that no ships are built now and to mend them with ore. The shield mends by itself, anywhere, at a rate the shield pips set (Ship systems, below); mining works anywhere. The price of a whole hull, of a point of a ship you protect and of a fighter's mend are three rows on the tuning panel (`N`, the ore group).

**A level with no outposts** (the alien home world; the rule is the level's own outpost list, `campAboard(C)` in `src/campoutpost.js`) has no dock row: the dock and repair list is Repair the hull alone, which works there as it does everywhere, and the retreat line is never said. Sol (every level that lists outposts) has the dock row first; **Alpha Centauri lists one, the gate at the relic**.

**Outposts are data.** `CAMP.levels[n].outposts = { <place id>: { name?, range? } }`: the Earth level has Earth, the Lunar base and the Mars base; the belt Occator Works (Ceres), Vesta Depot, Earth and the Mars base; Alpha Centauri Proxima Gate (at Proxima b) and the Alpha B relay (at Alpha Centauri B, added by card `space-game-outpost-hot-gate`: the guards of mission 2 sit round Proxima b, so while they live the gate's doors are shut and the relay is the retreat); the home world none (the only place is the enemy's: fight on, mending the hull in space). The map marks each with a green frame and a beacon, and the nav list flags it (`outpost: true`). The numbers are `CAMP.outpost` (`range` 280 px from the edge of the place to dock, `clear` 900 px without an alien near, `retreatHull` 0.35; the repair prices are `CAMP.mend`). Fast travel and pursuit (3-GEN) use the same list: `campOutposts(level)`, `campOutpostNear(C)`, `campOutpostNearest(C)` in `src/campoutpost.js`.

**Docking.** In range of an outpost, the row Dock at the outpost of the dock and repair list (`U`, then Enter: it is the first row) or "dock at Luna" (typed or spoken) docks: the carrier stops, the battle is paused and **the ready room opens as that outpost** (`src/campdock.js` registers the rows; `src/camphubscene.js` is the room): REPAIR (R mends the hull at once, at half price, for ore in proportion to what is missing), MISSIONS (the board), UNDOCK (Enter on it, or L; Esc and T too). A market outpost buys the ore in the store on the spot when you dock. Aliens within 900 px keep the doors shut ("Aliens too close to dock at Lunar base, pilot."), so docking is not a free breather in a fight. Builds are queued as ever and join the bay after their time, in flight.

**Ore and credits** (card `space-game-credits-apart-from-ore`; Troy, 22 Nov 2026). **Ore** is what the cruiser carries: 500 at the most (`CAMP.ore.cap`), mined and picked up in flight, spent anywhere (a repair in flight spends ore only). **Credits** are what an outpost pays for ore ("Sold 464 ore for 1392 credits."): no cap, saved beside the ore, and spent only at a station (docked at an outpost, the ready room's Refit, the Earth refit), where they are spent **first** and ore covers the rest. The prices did not change: a price is paid in credits, then ore, one for one. The HUD says `ORE 364/500   CREDITS 1392`; the ready room's Refit and the Earth refit show both. A mission starts with the save's ore in the store and its credits aboard, and ends with the ore left and all the credits (`campCarryOut`). An old save, where a sale's cash was saved as ore and cut to the store, is read with the ore over 500 as credits: nothing is lost. A refit item given back is refunded as credits. Rules: `campFunds`, `campPay` (a battle), `campWealth`, `campSpend`, `campRefund` (the save) in `src/campaign.js`; `tests/campcredits.mjs`. (It was one number until then: 1.2's sale of 464 ore for 1,392 began 1.3 with 500, and three warnings said so for a few hours: card `space-game-l1-pacing-bugs`.)

**The Fabricator is gone** (card `space-game-repair-with-ore`): nothing builds ships, so there is nothing for it to lift. A save that still lists the tech loads and ignores it.

**The retreat.** Hull below 35 percent and no ship of the wing left (in the bay, out, launching or being mended): the HUD line under the goal says **"Retreat to Luna: 12 s on Fast."** (the nearest outpost by the edge of its place, the flight the map would say on Fast), the ship says it once, and **the goal of the map and the edge arrow is that outpost** (the Fast button flies there). In range it says "P list: dock at Luna" instead. Typing "retreat to Luna" flies there; a bare "retreat" is still the wing's.

## How you play

> **Since card `space-game-cut-escorts` (22 Nov 2026) there is no escort ship.** Read every "escorts" of this section as the **squads**: the wing is three pilots, as strong as eight fighters (Red 3, Green 3, Yellow 2: see "Three pilots" below); "Escort the convoy" is an order of the all-squads list and of each squad's list; the Escorts list, Build an escort and the Scout order are gone (see "No escort ship" below, and README.md for the keys as they are now).

You fly the carrier yourself: big, slow, heavy. The **arrow keys** fly it (Up thrust, Left and Right turn, Down brake), hold **Space** to make its eight turrets fire at the ships close by, **Enter** fires a homing missile at the target (while no list is open), and the power list's last row Warp blink ahead blinks (once you have that tech; it was a row of the ship list, which is switched off). There is no other gun (see The weapons below). **Esc** closes a list or the map, else it pauses, with its own menu. Your small ships do the rest, and you give them orders from the **fleet list** and its parts (`Q` the whole wing, `E` the fighters, `R` the escorts, `W` the convoy; then Up or Down and Enter), by the **command strip** (`/`, then type) or by **voice** (hold `.`, then say it). The three ways run the same five orders:

| Order | Row, and the key of its list | You can type or say | What the small ships do |
|---|---|---|---|
| Launch | no key and no row (card `space-game-keys-orders`: the three pilots start each mission out beside the cruiser, and an order to a pilot in the bay launches it) | "launch", "launch fighters" | the bay opens: one ship out every 0.6 s (what the strip and the voice still do for pilots in the bay) |
| Attack | `E` with an enemy picked (the one order key: an enemy picked, attack it; a friendly, guard it; nothing, form up on the cruiser; "E is the one order key" below) | "attack", "attack the mothership" | fighters go for the target you picked (`[` `]` `S`); in the W list the row is grey until one is picked (card `space-game-w-uses-selection`: no list to choose from; `E` picks the nearest for you); typed, the nearest alien, or the mothership if you name it |
| Escort | Escort the convoy (`R`, the escorts) | "send escorts to the Mars convoy" | escorts fly beside the convoy you name (or the nearest one) and shoot what comes at it; in a level with no convoys (the belt) they stay close to the cruiser |
| Guard | Guard the cruiser, or Guard the target (the ship you protect that `A` `D` picked: "Guard the target" below) | "guard the carrier", "defend Earth", "red squad guard the transport" | each squad flies a flight in formation beside what you name, at its speed and heading (see Flights) |
| Recall | no key (the pilots stay out all mission); the Fast burn recalls them itself, and the strip and the voice still have it | "recall", "come home" | they fly back, land in the bay and are repaired; a ship lost stays lost; a ship still waiting to leave the bay (Launch empties it one ship every 0.6 s) goes back in the bay and does not come out later |

**Every mark of the map is a target (card `space-game-campaign-map-targets`, Troy, 20 Nov 2026).** A click on ANY mark of the Tab map sets it, as a click on a place did: your own ships (each one of the wing, and "Your ships"), the convoys, every alien ("Raider 3", "Gunship 2", "Drone 5"), the mothership and the towers, the ore and wrecks lying about, the rocks ("Rock 4"), the caches, scan points and relic, and the places (planets, outposts). Marks for the rocks (grey) and the ore (gold) are drawn for it. The set mark has its ring, the line from the carrier, its distance and the two buttons, Fly and Fast, with Enter and Shift+Enter; the pursuit rules are as before (a burn with the wing out tows it, card `space-game-burn-with-wing-out`). The nav list is the same rows by keys: goal, places, convoys, enemies, caches and relic, ore and rocks, your ships, nearest first in each (`campNavRows`, src/campnav.js). **The pick** (`campNavPickStack`): the nearest mark to the cursor within `CAMP.nav.pick` px; two marks on one spot are a stack, and a second click on the same spot (within `stackSame` px) takes the next one of it (a lone mark flies on the second click, as it did). **A moving target is led and followed** (`campNavLead`, `campNavDrive`): the carrier aims where the ship will be, closes at the Long Haul autopilot's rule in the target's frame (a ship faster than the carrier is chased at its top speed), and at the **stand-off range** keeps station with its speed matched, following until the trip is ended: `CAMP.nav.stopEnemy` 400 px from an alien (turret range), `stopFriend` 130 alongside your ship or a convoy, `stopRock` 200 from a rock (inside the mining laser), a point 40 (ore: pickup range), the mothership and a tower `stopShip` 450 as before. A target that dies (or a rock that breaks) ends the trip: "Autopilot off: Raider 3 is gone."; ore that is taken is "arrived". When the map is opened during a trip its place is set again with the buttons and the active one marked. No attack orders here (commanding the wing from the map is the next card). The names are in content/text/campmaptargets.md.

**A burn takes the wing with it (card `space-game-burn-with-wing-out`, ORCH for Troy, 23 Nov 2026; it was "Fast needs the wing aboard", card `space-game-campaign-fast-board`, Troy, 20 Nov 2026).** The burn (the map's Fast button, Shift+Enter, "fly fast to Mars", the cruiser list's Fly to the goal on a long leg, or the autopilot list's rows, `O`, Fast burn to the goal and Fast burn to the target) is `CAMP.nav.fast` = 2.2 times the carrier's speed (462 px/s, 672 on a far trip), above a fighter's 430, so the small ships cannot keep up on their own. The first rule was that the wing had to be aboard (a first press with a ship out was refused, a second recalled the wing and burned when the last ship landed); the pilots now start every mission out and no key recalls them, so that rule could never be met and **it is gone: a burn runs with the wing out and tows it.** While a burn flies (`C.nav.fast`), every ship of the wing with no hostile within `CAMP.flight.self` (450 px) of it is **towed** (`campTowSet`, `campTowTop` in `src/campaign.js`; the rule is written in `src/campnav.js`): it falls in on its slot in the cruiser's formation (`campFlightSlot`) whatever its order is (hold, mine, attack, fetch, escort: the order is kept and comes back when the burn ends), and may fly `CAMP.nav.tow` (1.3) times the cruiser's burn speed to catch up (`u.tow` is the factor on its top speed and push, put on by `campPaceShip`: about 600 px/s on a near burn, 874 on a far one), so the pilots keep formation and arrive with the cruiser. A recall or a repair run during a burn lands on the burning cruiser (`campWingHome` matches its velocity). A ship with a hostile that near is not towed: it fights as its order says and falls in again when it is clear. Nothing is refused, nothing waits, launching during a burn does not drop it, and `campNavFly` (src/campnav.js, the one door) just goes. The numbers `recallWait` and `askFor`, the state `C.navWait` and `C.fastAsk` and seven lines of words are gone. Tests: `tests/campburntow.mjs`. The edge arrow has no action, so it has nothing to obey. **Mid-flight (Troy, 20 Nov 2026): the two map buttons, Enter and Shift+Enter stay live for the whole trip.** A place you fly to stays set with its Fly and Fast buttons beside it; the one that is on is marked ON; a press on the other changes the speed of the same trip (`campNavGo` keeps the trip object and its start when the place is the same, so nothing is re-planned), and the switch to Fast burns at once with the wing out or aboard (the wing is towed). The set place and its buttons go when the trip ends (arrived, X, a steering key). Long Haul's sector map does the same by being opened again mid-flight (its destination is kept, its buttons are live, Fly or Fast is marked ON, Shift+Enter makes the same trip Fast); a trip already in **cruise** cannot be switched back to regular (the game says "Cruising already. S cancels.").

**Pursuit: a burn is a retreat (card `space-game-campaign-pursuit`, Troy, 20 Nov 2026).** Every alien's top speed is under the burn's 462 px/s (raider 430, drone 260, gunship 150; the mothership 55 and the towers do not chase) and over the carrier's regular 210 for the raider and the drone, so at regular speed they keep up and on Fast they fall behind. An alien that has been on the carrier (it targeted it, within its **leash**: `CAMP.alien.pursuit.leashOf` 1.25 times its firing range, so 650 px for a raider, 810 a drone, 975 a gunship) and then stays beyond the leash for `loseAfter` (1.5 s) **drops the chase** (`campPursuit` in src/campaign.js) and the ship says "Lost them." (once for a pack: `sayEvery` 8 s). A guard goes back to its post and holds there again; in a level with no Earth the rest come to rest where they are and stay out of it until the carrier comes within the leash again; in the Earth level a wave goes on to Earth as it was. One that was never near (it is coming from afar) has nothing to lose and keeps coming; one that is hit, or sees one of your other ships in reach, is on it again. The raider is only 32 px/s under the burn, so a short burn (under about 5 s) does not shake one: a trip to somewhere a few thousand px off does (the far burn, 672, certainly).

**One list per hand, with shortcut keys (cards `one-list-keys`, Troy, 22 Nov 2026, and `list-shortcuts`, Troy, 23 Nov 2026).** The first card replaced the two always-on panels, their pages and their one-key orders with one flat list per hand. The second ("shortcut keys that take you straight to the sub-list") cut each list into parts and gave the parts keys of their own. **`Q` opens the fleet list** at the left, **`P` opens the ship list** at the right (both lists are switched off now: `Q` picks all three pilots, `P` opens the power list: see "P opens power" below). Each is short: a few orders, and one row for each of its parts, with the part's key in a cap at the row's left and an arrow after its words. **A part's key opens its list directly, at any time:** `W` all squads, `E` the red squad, `R` the green squad, `T` the yellow squad on the left; `P` power (Shields first: `P`, then Enter, adds a shield pip; it was `O` until card `space-game-p-opens-power`, Troy, 23 Nov 2026, and `O` took the autopilot with card `space-game-autopilot-on-o`), `O` autopilot (it was `I`), `U` dock and repair on the right. So `W`, then Enter, sends every fighter at the target and `E`, then Enter, only the red squad. The escorts and the convoy have no key: their rows in the `Q` list open them (`Q`, Escorts; `Q`, Convoy). Nothing is on the screen until one of these keys is pressed, and only one list is open at a time. In an open list **Up and Down** move the highlight (round at the ends) and **Enter** gives the highlighted order. (The **S list is the exception since card `space-game-s-list-numbers`**: a list marked `numbers: 1` shows a number chip on each row and does the row of the digit you press, `S` then `1` to `7`; it takes neither Up, Down, Left, Right nor Enter, so the arrows fly the ship and Enter fires a missile with it open; a greyed row's digit says why and flashes it; `Esc`, `S` or the fade close it; its target sub-list is numbered too.) On a part's row **Enter, Right or a click** opens that part and gives no order. **Left** goes back to the list it is a part of, with the highlight on that part's row (in the `Q` and `P` lists it does nothing). The list's own key closes it, and **Esc goes back one step, to where you came from** (a list a row opened goes back to `Q` or `P`; a list its own key opened closes; "Picking the target of an order"); another list's key opens that list in its place. An order that is taken closes the list; one that is refused leaves it open and says why. A pip row and the balance row of the power list leave it open (`stay`), to be given again. A list opened by its key or from its part's row opens on its first row (All squads: its first row that can be given). Nothing cycles: each convoy order is a plain row. FLEET ORDERS (`Q`): All squads, Red squad, Green squad, Yellow squad, Escorts, Convoy (six rows, every one a part that opens its list; the list is as tall as its rows; Launch, Guard, Mine and Recall are not here, they are in All squads: nothing is listed twice; card `space-game-squad-tint`, Troy, 22 Nov 2026). ALL SQUADS (`W`): Launch the wing, Attack the target (none picked: the one hostile, or a list of them to choose from: "Picking the target of an order"), Guard the cruiser, Recall, Mine ore. It opens on its first row that can be given (`start: 'usable'` in the table): Launch while ships are in the bay and, once the wing is out (Launch greyed, "all out"), Attack the target. Attack, Guard and Recall of the `E`, `R` and `T` lists and the escorts' list go to that part only (a row with `wing: [group, verb]`, given through `campWingOrder`; Attack goes at the picked target, with none picked the nearest enemy). **A row that can never be used in a mission is left out** (its `when`: the Convoy row of the `Q` list with no convoy and no escorted ship, the blink without its tech, the build rows where ships are built docked). The four convoy rows are always in the `W` list: with no convoy they are grey and say so. **A row that cannot be used now stays, grey, with the reason beside it.** The convoy order in force is marked, and the Convoy row of the `Q` list names it. A list is as tall as its rows (its box is 360 px wide). One longer than `CAMP_PANELS.show` = 9 rows would scroll with the highlight; none is that long today. Its head has the list's key, its title and the highlighted row's number; its foot says the highlighted row's state or its reason, and the keys (with Left for back in a part's list). **The direct keys are only these**, and none means two things in flight: the **arrow keys** fly (Up thrust, Left and Right turn, Down brake; `W A S D` do not), **Space** fires the turrets, **Enter** fires a missile at the target (only while no list is open), `A` `D` `S` `X` are the target, `J` `K` `L` `;` the power (the `O` list has the same four orders as rows), **Tab** the map, `-` `=` and **Backspace** the zoom, `H` the page of keys, `M` mute, **Esc** (close a list or the map, else pause), **`/`** the strip and **`.`** the voice (the Long Haul keeps its own keys untouched). ## Dogfights: fighters fly like Vipers (card `space-game-fighter-dogfights`; Troy, 21 Nov 2026: "the fighters should fight more, like the Vipers in Battlestar Galactica: as manoeuvrable, and dogfight like that")

**Who.** The wing's fighters, the aliens' raiders and the pirates' **skiffs** (a pirate whose base is the raider: `CAMP.pirate.kinds`; the rim run's level 1 has pirates and no raiders), against **each other** only. A drone, a cutter, a gunship, a brute, an escort, the mothership, a hauler, a neutral, the carrier and Earth are not dogfights: a ship that flies at one of those is fought by the wing's fighters in strafing runs (`campStrafe`, `src/campflight.js`; see Flights), and by the aliens with the pirates' own circle and pass (`pirateSteer`, `src/enemies.js`). **The wing's orders still win**: the foe a fighter flies at is the one its order names (an Attack on a named alien is that alien; on "the nearest", or on guard, it is the ship's partner when the nearest is a raider).

**The flight** (`src/campdogfight.js`, numbers in `CAMP.dog`). The engine pushes **along the nose**: a push backwards has `retro` (0.45) of the accel, a push sideways `side` (0.35). So a ship turns to where it means to burn, and one that wants to stop turns its nose round and burns (the **flip and burn**: nose on the target, the main engine against the way it was going, firing as it comes round). A dogfighter keeps more of its speed than the game's ships (`drag` 0.62 a second, the game's own is 0.5), so it drifts and overshoots, and its nose turns `snap` (1.7) times as fast for a break, a flip or a jink. Pace scales speed, accel and turn as it always did. **The pace of it:** a fighter or a raider in a fight flies, turns and fires at `CAMP.pace.fight` (0.5) of its pace (card `space-game-slower-fights`; `campFightPace` in `src/campaign.js`): the same duel lasts twice as long. It takes hold when a foe is within 900 px and is gone by 1,700 px.

**The moves**, each with a time drawn from the battle's own seeded numbers (a seed repeats a fight exactly):

| Move | When | What |
|---|---|---|
| approach | farther than `far` (1,100 px) | burn in on the lead point |
| attack | in range | nose on the lead point (the aim is `lead` of the exact lead: the wing 0.8, the raiders 0.55), the push to the speed that closes on the target (never under `pass`, so it drives in for a pass); the nose goes to the push instead when it is more than `cone` off the aim (a ship sliding round its target turns to kill the slide) |
| break | inside `near` (120 px): it is past | a hard turn `angle` off the line, 0.5 to 0.9 s, then round again |
| flip | the target is more than `flip.off` behind the nose, inside `flip.range`, once in `flip.every` s | the turn on the spot and the burn |
| jink | any enemy that has its nose on it (`jink.aspect`, within `jink.range`: not only its partner, a drone's guns hurt too) | a hard turn square to that enemy, a chance of `rate` a second (the wing 2, the raiders 0.8), once in `every` s |

**The pairs** (`campDogPair`, every `pair.every` ms): each fighter and raider within `pair.range` px is matched with the nearest unmatched ship of the other side, nearest pairs first, and the pair holds until one is lost or they are `pair.keep` px apart: a duel, not a crowd on one ship. More raiders than fighters: the extra raiders have no partner and go on as the pirates did.

**What it did to the numbers** (tools: a duel harness in the card's review; `node tools/campaign-bot.mjs` for the table). One fighter against one raider at Normal: before, half of them never ended (they circled at the ring and missed); now every one ends, in 3 to 70 s, the middle one in about 19 s, with 2 or 3 flips, 8 breaks and 7 jinks. Three against three: decided in about 25 s. The wing takes about the hit points it did (the home level's plain player: 149 against 135 in 200 s) and deals about 28 percent more, because a fighter now goes for a partner and not for the crowd; the missions' bot table is as it was (every run won, the times within 10 percent). Three things the first version got wrong and the numbers now guard: the wing walked past a drone to a duel and the haulers died of it (a partner only replaces a nearest foe that is itself a dogfighter); a fighter that flew straight at a circling drone was hit three times as often (drones and gunships are not dogfights); a break of over a second took a duel across the map (0.5 to 0.9 s).

**Pictures.** The wing's fighter (`campFighterDraw`, `src/campcruiser.js`) and the raider (`shRaiderDraw`, `src/ships.js`) are one drawn shape in two colour schemes: a long needle of a nose, a narrow blade of a body, two short fins forward, two swept wings reaching to the tail, a gun in front of each wing and **three engines in a row at the stern** (the raider has three flames, `SHIPS.nozzles.raider`). An original drawing: nothing of the show's ship is copied, no name or mark. The fighter keeps the muted steel and bronze with the pale canopy; the raider the pirates' dark red and hot pink.

**Not done (parked).** The drone and the gunship keep their old circling (they are not fighters); the Long Haul's own pirates (`pirateAI`, Phaser physics) are unchanged; no sound for a flip.

## Repair: a hurt fighter lands and comes back (card `space-game-fighter-repair`; Troy, 21 Nov 2026: "fights were not interactive and escorts were boring, I want to direct the fleet more")

**The rule** (`src/camprepair.js`, numbers in `CAMP.repair`). A **fighter** of the wing under **25 percent** of its hull breaks off, flies home by itself, lands on the cruiser, is mended for **20 s**, at no ore, and goes out again whole, with the order it had (card `space-game-keys2-orders`; it was 30 percent, 10 s and 5 ore; the first two are rows of the N panel). If the cruiser is more than **15 s of flight** away (the way over the fighter's own top speed) it fights on, and breaks off when the cruiser comes within reach. Every level, pirates and aliens alike. The enemy's fighters never do it (the wing's AI calls it first; the raiders' and the pirates' AI do not). The escorts and the scouts do not break off.

**A radio line each way** (the ship's voice, `content/text/campaign-repair.md`): "Fighter hit hard. Breaking off to land." and "Fighter mended and launching again." (or "Fighter mended. It is in the bay." when it stays aboard). Four fighters hurt in one pass are one call (`sayEvery` 5 s). **The wing strip** (the bay line's column of the HUD) shows `REPAIR 2  7 s`: how many are being mended and the seconds until the first is whole. The ship computer's status adds "n being mended".

**Out again.** When the 20 s are over the fighter goes out at once, fight or none, with the order it had (keys2-orders: the pilots stay out all mission, there is no Launch and no recall key). Only the **Fast burn** keeps one aboard: its recall holds a pilot being mended in the bay when it is whole, and E (or any order) launches it afterwards.

**Orders on the way home.** The repair wins over an order: an Attack given to a fighter that is flying home is kept, and is the order it goes out with; a Recall given to it (the Fast burn's) is kept too. A fighter **above** 25 percent that is recalled lands as it always did: whole at once, in the bay. Under 25 percent a recalled fighter is mended for the 20 s like any hurt one.

**What it counts as.** A fighter being mended is still one of the wing: the build limit counts it, a dock counts it, "no ships left" does not say so. It is not in `C.ships` (nothing can hit it, find it or count it as out), as a landed ship never is; `C.repairs` holds it (`{ kind, until, order, stay }`). `C.mended` counts the fighters made whole (the bot table has it as `mended` in `--json`).

**What it did to the numbers** (`node tools/campaign-bot.mjs --seeds 6`). Every bot still wins every mission it won, in about the same time, with **fewer ships lost** (the plain bot's level 1.3 and 3.x runs lose 1 to 2 fighters where they lost 3 to 8) and the carrier hurt a little more or less as the fights fall. Level 3 of the pinned fight (tests/campsystems.mjs B, tests/campscouts.mjs K) ends in another state: 40 turret shots where there were 98, as the wing kills the eight sooner. The one check that moved is tests/campaign.mjs's "still not a walk": the same player on the old harsh numbers (Earth's shield 300, no mending) won one seed of six before the easier card and wins five of six now (it lost three of them by Earth's shield); the guard is now "not all six, and at least one lost by Earth's shield". If the repair is too kind, the levers are `below` (0.3), `time` (10) and `within` (15).

**Not done (parked).** An outpost's repair bay does not mend fighters faster; the escorts do not repair; no picture of a fighter on the landing deck, and the landing lights use the recall's (a hurt fighter flying home lights the bays as a recalled one does); the map's wing list does not show the ships being mended.

## The weapons (card `space-game-campaign-missiles`)

The carrier has two ways to hurt an alien, one key each (card `space-game-campaign-turrets` took away the single main gun):

| Weapon | How | What it is for |
|---|---|---|
| Turrets | hold **Space**: eight turrets spread along the hull (four a side, two near the nose, two at the stern, rows of `CAMP.carrier.mounts`); each aims itself at the nearest alien within 500 px of it (to the edge of the alien), always, and fires one shot of 4 hit points every 0.7 s, but only while Space is held and only at something in reach; nothing fires on its own. The eight do not fire together: each has a slot of its own in the 0.7 s cycle, a turret's width of the cycle (about 90 ms) behind the one before it, moved a little at random (12 ms), so the fire is a continuous rattle and not a volley; a slot with nothing to shoot at, or with Space up, is let go, so nothing bursts when Space is pressed. Each turret leads its shot: it points where its shot and the alien will meet (the alien's speed, the shot's 800 px/s and the carrier's own drift), so a gunship crossing at 100 px/s or a raider at its top speed is hit, not just a tower that stands still | the small ships that come close, and any big one you fly up to; 46 hit points a second in all |
| Missile launcher | **Enter** (while no list is open); "fire missiles" or "missiles at the mothership" typed on the strip (`/`) or spoken (hold `.`) | big ships: the mothership, the towers and the gunships |

On the screen each turret is a dark base with an amber dot; once it has picked an alien it points a short barrel at it (even with Space up), and when it shoots it flashes white with a longer barrel. A faint ring round the carrier shows how far they reach, and their shots are green, like the pilots' (`CAMP_SHOT.earth`, the texture `shot-earth`; card `space-game-fleet-lasers-green`; the pirates' are amber, the aliens' red, the patrols' cyan; the beam tech's keep a cyan tint). Each shot has a muzzle flash (a short spark at the barrel tip) and a quiet blip whose pitch changes from turret to turret, at most one blip in 70 ms, so eight turrets rattle and do not stack into noise.

A missile is a homing round. It goes for the mothership if the mothership is within 2,400 px, else the nearest tower or gunship within that reach; named ("at the mothership") it goes for the mothership only, and says so if the mothership is not there or too far. It leaves the tube slowly, gets up to speed (560 px/s) and turns at 3.4 rad/s, so one fired the wrong way still comes round. It carries 7 seconds of fuel, then it dies with no bang (a plain puff). Its fuze goes off near a big ship only: a raider or a drone on its way does not stop it. Where it goes off, every alien within 80 px is hurt: a big ship 100 hit points, any other alien 10 (a raider has 20, a drone 30). Six missiles take 600 of the mothership's 700 (see `CAMP.mother.hp`), the guns and the wing finish it; a tower (160) takes two; a gunship (80) one. A smoke trail follows it and the bang is a plain explosion.

The magazine is small and a missile **costs ore** (card `space-game-missiles-cost-ore`, Troy, 24 Nov 2026): you start with 6, and **nothing fills it for free**. At **0** the ship builds ONE missile by itself, if the store holds `CAMP.missile.cost` (20) ore: the ore is paid when the build starts and the missile is in the rack 10 s later (`CAMP.missile.reload`: 15 s until card `space-game-missile-build-10s`, Troy, 24 Nov 2026, "missiles should build in 10 seconds"; the N panel's row Missile build time moves it). **R** (the key `CAMP_KEYS.reload`, the S list's row **Reload missiles** with the R chip and what it would cost, the words "reload missiles", the ship computer's act `fleet_reload`) **queues ONE missile a press** (card `space-game-missile-queue`, Troy, 24 Nov 2026: "hitting R multiple times shows me how many are queued"; it used to build the whole rack in one press): presses add up, at most to the empty slots of the rack, and the queue is built one after another, 20 ore each taken when its build starts, on the same build time, so a calm moment can fill the rack with a few presses. A press is refused in words when the rack is full, when every empty slot already has a missile on its way ("Already building the rack"), or when the ore does not pay for it (the store must cover every missile still waiting, with the new one); if the ore is spent elsewhere later, the queue stops where it is and the radio says so once. Loot that fills the rack cuts the queue to the slots left. The state is `C.reloadAt` (the build under way) and `C.queued` (the missiles waiting for their build to start). The launcher cycles for 0.7 s between two launches. The count lives in the status block at the top right, over HOSTILES (card `space-game-ore-missiles-topright`) (`MISSILES  4/6` with the R key-cap chip beside it, in red when it is empty; `MISSILES  4/6 +2` while two are on their way, the one being built and the one waiting: the chip follows the words; `building 12 s` on its right while one is being built, `no ore` when it is at zero and nothing can be built). The count is one plain number, `C.missiles`; the loot of the resources card adds to it through `campMissileAdd(C, n)` (up to 12, the most the magazine holds) and costs no ore. The build is `src/campmissilebuild.js`; all the numbers are `CAMP.missile` in `src/campaign.js` (and the N panel's rows Missile build time and Ore for a missile). The key line and the HUD line name the key from the key table (`{keymissile}`); the ship computer has the order `fleet_missiles` (the model is told the key and the act in a battle).

## Anomalies: the cloaked caches (card `space-game-campaign-anomalies`; Troy: "spots where space looks odd and warped, like the Predator's invisibility")

Off the missions' routes there are spots where space looks warped: **cloaked enemy caches**, a store of the aliens' weapons or a lab of their tech. The cloak fools the sensors (no contact, no name, no blip) but not the eye: it shows as an **anomaly**, a patch of heat-haze, and nothing is known about it until you come. The table is `CAMP.levels[n].anomalies` (the belt five: three in the belt, one round Mars and one round Jupiter's moon Io, a Fast trip of under a minute; Alpha Centauri and the home world three each); the numbers are `CAMP.anomaly`; the code is `src/campanomaly.js` (the sim, no Phaser) and `src/campanomalydraw.js` (the haze).

1. **Noticed.** The carrier within `sense` (2,400 px; the Lens tech doubles it) notices it once: the ship says "Odd readings ahead", the radio calls it, and it has a mark on the **Tab map** (broken rings and a question mark, in the legend as Anomalies) and a row in the **nav list** named "Anomaly" with its distance. Nothing says what it hides. The words find it: "fly to the anomaly".
2. **The haze.** In the game view it is a layer of broken rings that ripple outward, a faint lens and the stars behind it pulled and doubled, drawn from `haze` (1,500 px), stronger as the carrier nears; no shader, no asset (the graphics' own calls, alpha at most 0.55). The ring of the visit fills while the carrier holds there.
3. **The visit.** The carrier within `visit` (260 px) for `hold` (2) seconds decloaks it (the count falls back when it leaves). The autopilot stops at `stop` (160 px), inside it, so "Fast" there is enough. The flash, then the cache is drawn (a ring with crates and a dish), the ship says what it is, and its **cloaked guards** come out on a ring `ring` px round it and go for the carrier (the row's `guards`: raiders, drones, gunships).
4. **The loot, once.** The last guard dead, a beat later the reward: **ore** as chunks lying on the cache (`chunk` ore each, any ship picks them up), **a heavier weapon** (more missiles in the magazine, up to `missile.max`: `C.magazine`; a heavier turret: `C.turretMul`, times the turrets' damage), and **a tech item** that the Earth refit offers as one more choice (Overdrive: the carrier a quarter faster; Lens: anomalies noticed twice as far). The loot is saved with the progress (`P.found`: `'<level>.<id>'`) and **does not come back**; the weapons stay with the pilot (each battle begins with the magazine and the turrets of everything looted so far, read from the saved keys).
5. **The refit.** The Earth refit lists what was looted after the three techs, **numbered 6, 7 and 8** (4 and 5 keep buying ships); the plain pick after the later levels lists them as 4 and on. What was not picked is offered again at the next pick.
6. **Bonus objectives.** A mission asks for one of its level's anomalies as a bonus, `{ id: 'find', type: 'anomaly', anomaly: '<id>' }` ("Find the cloaked cache"): met when the cloak is down in that flight, or when it was looted before (it was found). The score, the debrief and the ranks use it like any bonus. Nine missions, nine anomalies; the Mars and Io ones are for the loot alone.

## Wing orders with groups and targets (card `space-game-campaign-wing-targets`; Troy: "command the fleet from the map")

## Sensors: seeing hostiles from afar (card `space-game-see-hostiles-earlier`; Troy, 22 Nov 2026: "see hostiles earlier so I can send squads to meet them")

- **What is seen.** `CAMP.sensor.range` (9,000 px, a row of the `N` tuning panel) is how far from the cruiser a hostile counts as *seen*; inside the sensor circle of a scout that is out (`CAMP.scout.sense`, 2,600 px) a hostile is seen at any distance, so a scout sent ahead sees further. The mothership and the towers (`CAMP.sensor.always`) are always seen: they are the mission's own marks. Everything the player can look at or pick goes through `campSeen` (`src/campsight.js`): the Tab map's marks, the nav list's enemy rows and the target keys (`A` `D` `S`), the edge arrows and the radio's call. The AIs, the turrets and the sim never read it: a hostile out of sight still flies and fires.
- **Why 9,000.** It is 21 s of the fastest raider's flight (430 px/s) and 35 s of a drone's (260). ## Health bars on the ships you protect (cards `space-game-protected-health` and `space-game-health-bar-quiet`; Troy, 22 Nov 2026: "a small health bar on every ship I protect", then "so they stay out of the way")

- **Who has one.** The ships you protect: `campConvoyShips` (`src/campconvoy.js`), the same ships the convoy orders and the *Escort the convoy* sub-list name. A neutral that is not your charge (a patrol), your fighters and escorts, the aliens and the cruiser have none; a ship that is lost or has arrived loses its bar.
- **Quiet: a thin line, a full bar under attack.** Normally the bar is a **thin line** over the ship (`CAMP.charge.line.h`, 2 px), **full length at full hull** (`CAMP.charge.bar.w`, 38 px) and, when the ship is hurt, **shortened and coloured** to the hull that is left, with no number; a faint track behind it shows how much is gone. For `CAMP.charge.loud` ms (**4 s**) after the last hit the ship takes, it is the **full bar** (5 px, a dark frame) **with its percentage** at the right of it; then it **eases back** to the line over `CAMP.charge.ease` (0.5 s), not a pop. A new hit makes it full again at once. "Under attack" is the ship's own `hurtAt` (the tick of the last `campHurt`), so a miner the pirates are harrying keeps its full bar and a ship tucked under the cruiser's shield (the hit is the cruiser's) stays a thin line. The share of full (`loud`, 0 to 1) is a function of the time since the hit, so it needs no memory (`campChargeLoud`).
- **Colour and flash.** The hull as a share of its most: **green** above 60 percent (`CAMP.charge.amber`), **amber** from 60 down to 30 (`CAMP.charge.red`), **red** under that, and **white for 700 ms** (`CAMP.charge.flash`) after a hit. It measures the hull only: these ships have no shields and none was added.
- **Where.** In the flight view, over the ship, at the same size on the screen at every zoom (it is its own graphics object scaled by 1 over the zoom, so the far view's ships held at their least size, `CAMPZOOM.view.leastSmall` and `leastBig`, still have a line you can read; the gap is from the ship's drawn edge, never less than 14 px; the line is at the bottom, the full bar grows upward). On the Tab map, a 16 px line over the ship's mark that is the 3 px bar while the ship is under attack (**no number on the map**). In the *Escort the convoy* sub-list the row keeps its plain number (km and percent) and a bar at the right of it, always full size: the sub-list is a deliberate look. The *Attack* and *Mine ore* sub-lists have none.
- **Cheap.** A bar is drawn again only when what it shows changes (its fill in px, its colour band, the size's eighth, the flash's fifth); each frame only its place moves. The percentage is made the first time a ship is hit (a ship nobody hits has no text object) and set through `campLabel` (`src/camptextguard.js`), so it is painted again only when its words or colour change. The bars are rectangles: nothing here draws a circle.
- **Built.** `CAMP.charge` (`src/campaign.js`), `src/campcharge.js` (the rule: `campChargeHull`, `campChargeBand`, `campChargeLoud`, `campChargeLook`, `campChargeShips`, `campChargeBars`, `campChargeMark`; pure), `src/campchargescene.js` (the flight view's bars, kept in `scene.hud.charge`), the map mark's `hull`, `band`, `hit` and `loud` (`src/campmap.js`, drawn in `src/campmapdraw.js`), the sub-list row's `hull` and `band` (`src/camppick.js`, drawn in `src/campanelsdraw.js`), `tests/campcharge.mjs`. ## Convoy orders: tuck in, hold here, run for it (card `space-game-convoy-orders`; Troy, 21 Nov 2026: "escorts were boring; I want to direct the fleet more")

The escorted ships (the transport, the haulers, the neutral miners) take orders now, to all of them or to one by name. The undo anchor is the git tag `fleet-command-before`. Every level, pirates and aliens alike.

- **Tuck in.** The ship flies to the cruiser and stays inside its shield bubble (`bubble` 120 px). A hit on a tucked ship inside the bubble is a hit on the cruiser: the shield takes it, then the hull (shots, torpedoes and their blast; `campHurt` asks `campConvoyCover`). The convoy ship goes on along its road, but only while it stays inside the bubble, so the cruiser sets the pace; a tucked miner lies under the cruiser in a slot of its own (`slots`). The price is the cruiser's shield and being tied to the convoy.
- **Hold here.** It brakes (`accel`, about a second) and waits where it is, with no cover. A held ship never arrives. A miner stops working.
- **Run for it.** Full speed to its goal (`run`, 1.6 times its speed; a miner goes home), with no cover: it is out from under the shield and the escorts must keep the way clear. It still arrives and counts as delivered.
- **Carry on.** Back to its own course: the order is cancelled.
- **The hull blocks.** A shot or a torpedo flies on until it touches a ship, so a cruiser between the shooter and the ship behind it is the first it touches: shots and torpedoes aimed at a transport behind the cruiser hit the cruiser (the test holds it).
- **Where.** `campOrder(C, 'convoy', words)` is the one door: the words say the verb and may name a ship ("tuck in Hauler 2"). The four rows are always in the convoy list: with no convoy they are grey and say "no convoy" (and there is no way in: the Convoy row is out of `Q`). The Convoy row of the `Q` list is left out in a mission with no convoy and no escorted ship. The strip and the ship's voice: "tuck in", "tuck in close", "take cover", "run for it", "carry on", and hold with a convoy word ("haulers hold", "hold the transport", "stop the convoy"): a plain "hold here" is not a convoy order. The list's key and the rows' words are read from `CAMP_PANELS`, never typed.
- **What you see.** While any ship is tucked in, the cruiser's bubble is drawn (a soft ring) with a thin link to each tucked ship (dashes when the ship is outside it: no cover); a hit taken for a tucked ship flashes a line from it to the cruiser. The radio says the order ("Convoy, tuck in under our shield.", "Hauler 2, run for it. No cover.").

## Scouting the route: a fork in an escort's road (card `space-game-scout-route`; Troy, 21 Nov 2026: "fights were not interactive and escorts were boring; I want to direct the fleet more") **DORMANT: since card `space-game-cut-lane-fork` (Troy, 22 Nov 2026: "remove picking a lane, not feeling it") no mission row has a `route`.** Everything below still works for a row that writes one (`tests/camproute.mjs` flies a fixture mission with a fork); the Tab map's key T that took a lane, the hints "pick a lane" and "the transport waits at the fork" and the briefing's fork lines were removed, so a mission that uses the system again needs those back (git history: the commit before the cut).
On an escort mission the road **forks**. A scout sent ahead (the Scout order, V on the Tab map) finds what waits on each lane: an ambush on the short one, a quiet long one, sometimes a cache. The player **picks the lane** (the Tab map's Take button, then also key T, the words "take the long lane"). Unscouted, the ambush is a surprise. It is mission **data**, so any escort mission of any level, pirates or aliens, can have one. The code is `src/camproute.js` (pure, no Phaser; `tests/camproute.mjs`); the numbers are `CAMP.route` in `src/campaign.js`.
- **The row** (a mission row's `route`): `{ fork, join, wait, pick, convoy, lanes: [{ id, name, bend, hides: { waves, caches } }] }`. `fork` and `join` are shares (0 to 1) of the straight road where the lanes part and meet; `bend` is how far a lane's middle is off the road in px (0: the road itself; minus is to the right of the way she flies), so the lane's length follows from it; `wait` (s, `CAMP.route.wait` 45 when it says nothing) is how long the convoy holds at the fork for the player's word, and `pick` the lane she then takes (the first when it says nothing). `convoy` says which convoy the lanes are measured on (the first of the mission); **every** convoy of the mission takes the lane that is picked.
- **What a lane hides:** `hides.waves` are **ordinary wave rows** (so torpedo boats, pirates, aliens, `prey`, `breakOn` all work in them). `campMissionRow` lays them into the row's `waves` after the plain ones with the lane's id on each (`w.lane`); a wave of a lane comes only to a convoy on that lane. `on: { lane: f }` stays the share of the **whole** road, the same on every lane (the fork is at `fork` and the join at `join` however long the lane is), so a lane wave belongs between them and a plain wave outside them comes on every lane. `hides.caches` are `{ at: 0.5, off: 0, ore: 40 }`: the share of the lane, px to the side, and what the cache holds (the ore goes into the store when a ship of yours takes it, or a scout brings it home); they are in `C.caches` from the start.
- **The convoy** flies start - fork - the lane's middle - join - her place. At the fork, with no lane chosen, she **holds** (`campRouteHolding(C)` is `null` or `{ lanes, left, convoy }`) until the word comes, `wait` runs out (then the default lane, said "No word. Taking the ..."), or an enemy comes within `CAMP.route.panic` (1,400 px) of her (she does not sit still under fire: the default lane at once). The pick can be changed until she has left the fork, then it is locked ("Too late, pilot"). A ship put at her place arrives whatever the state of the fork.
- **The scout:** the lanes are rows of the Tab map's list (a group of their own, ROUTE; `lane:<id>`, at the lane's middle) and marks of the map (dashed, the chosen one firm green). The Scout order is allowed on them whatever the mission's `scouts` say (and the scout may fetch a cache that lies there). At the lane's middle it reads for `CAMP.scout.station` s and says what it found, from the data of the lane ("Scout 1, short lane: ambush, 3 pirates." / "quiet, a cache"); `C.route.known[id]` keeps it: `{ ships, pirates, aliens, caches }`.
- **The words:** `campOrder(C, 'lane', 'long')` (a lane's id, a word of its name or a map row; `campCheck` is the dry run; refused with `nolane`, `lanewhat`, `lanelocked`); the voice and the strip ("take the long lane", "go by the short lane", "long lane": `fleet_lane`; "scout the long lane": `fleet_scout`); `content/text/campaign-route.md`.
- **The bots** (`tools/campaign-bot.mjs`): a bot that launches sends a scout to each lane as soon as an escort is out and, when the transport holds at the fork (or has 8 s of the wait left), takes the lane with fewer pirates; the two passive bots of Troy's first try (`troy`, `troyh`) have `noroute: true` and let the default lane be taken for them.
- **1.1 had a fork** (the first user) until the cut: the **short lane** (straight) was the road it flies now, with the three raids the whole road had before folded into the ambush at 0.52 and the miner's call; the **long lane** bent 4,200 px off the road (a little under a minute longer), one small raid and a cache of 40 ore (120 cash at the Works). Both went away with the fork; the ambush, the timing and the other beats are as they were on the short lane.

## How it is built

- `src/campaign.js` is the whole game in plain numbers, no Phaser: the level table, the ships, the waves, the five orders, the aliens' flying (the game's own `pirateSteer` and `PIRATES` from `src/enemies.js`), win and lose, the tech pick and its save. `tests/campaign.mjs` runs whole battles of it in Node.
- `src/campaignscene.js` is a thin scene: it reads the keys, steps the game, and draws what the numbers say. It has the only Phaser in the mode and is not run by a test.
- The command strip, the ship computer and the voice reach it through the closed list (`SC_ACT`): `fleet_launch`, `fleet_attack`, `fleet_escort`, `fleet_guard`, `fleet_recall`. The words are read by the game itself, with no chat key needed.
- Story text is in `content/text/ui.md` (keys `camp.*`), so it can be translated or changed without code.

## Assumed, and open

- The playing field is a 1 to 12 scale of the real Sol distances (Earth to Mars is about 4,100 px), not the true scale: true scale would make a level an hour.
- The campaign has no money: the convoys that arrive only appear in the story line after the level.
- Alpha Centauri and Epsilon Eridani are my picks; the alien home world has no real name.
- The turrets' reach is 500 px, about three and a half carrier lengths (the carrier is drawn 98 px long). With the single gun gone, the carrier's only far reach is the missile. The numbers (500 px, 4 a shot, every 0.7 s, eight turrets: 46 a second) were fitted to the sensible players of `tests/campaign.mjs` twice: first with shots aimed where the alien is (80 a second), then, when `space-game-campaign-turret-lead` made the shots hit moving aliens, lowered, because a shot that lands is worth more than one that misses. A carrier that stays at the Moon and holds Space still wins level 1 in 2.5 to 5 minutes; with the old, harder numbers (Earth's shield 300, no mending) it wins at most half the seeds. One table, `CAMP.carrier`.
- Missiles are for big ships on purpose: with only raiders and drones near, the missile key answers "No big ship in missile range" and spends nothing.
- Left out of this cut: a map between levels, saving a level in the middle, difficulty, music of its own, and more than three techs a level.
