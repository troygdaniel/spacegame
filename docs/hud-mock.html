What changed

  • Shield and hull are two thin bars with an icon each. The numbers come back only when the ship is hurt.
  • The pilots are three rings. Each ring is that pilot's hull, the letter is the pilot, the digit under it is the key. Idle pilots dim, as resting agents do on the board.
  • The goal is one label and one bar. "Wave 1 of 5" and "dock at Occator Works" are gone until they matter.
  • Missiles are six pips. Ore is a bar.
  • Hostiles is the one lit panel, because it is the one thing that needs you. It replaces the radio sentence, the panel title and the edge label, which all said the same thing.
  • Empty panels are not drawn. Friendlies appears when you pick one.
  • The bottom key line is gone. Every key that matters is a chip on its panel.

What it would take

  • The HUD moves out of the game's canvas into page elements styled with CSS. Ships, fights and the map stay as they are.
  • No image files, no libraries, no build step. This page uses Inter from the web; the game would use the system typeface, which on a Mac is very close.
  • It replaces the HUD drawing code the local team has worked in all week, and the tests that pin where canvas text sits. That is the real cost.
  • The radio line, the briefing and the win card are separate screens and would follow the same pattern.

The game picture was taken in a cloud browser with software rendering, so colours and sharpness are not your Mac's. Leftover labels were painted out by hand in four places. Values (ore 120, three skiffs at 378 km, all pilots at full hull) are the real ones from that moment.
